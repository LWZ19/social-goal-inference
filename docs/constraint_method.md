# The constraint method

This page is for a reader of the repository who wants to know what the second method for the goal step does, how to run it against the baseline, where it is known to go wrong, and what has been measured. The commands are explained in the [README](../README.md). "The design" below is the project's design document for the method, which is not part of this repository.

The pipeline has one step where people decide the outcome: where the robot stops when the people at the target matter. The baseline has a model mark that place as a point on the image. The constraint method splits the step in two. A model says what the people require of the place, in a small fixed vocabulary, and geometry finds the place. It is chosen with `goal: constraints` and runs in exactly the episodes where the baseline runs: those in which the context check says that the people matter. Everything before that step is the same in both.

A figure for what the solver does is given on this page only where a test in `tests/` asserts it, and the file of the test is named, or where it is a count of recorded runs solved again without a model, and is said to be one ("Known limits", and "Measured on recorded runs" near the end). Nothing here is a result of the method on a model.

## What it does, in four steps

The README and the design count three steps, taking the first two below as one.

1. **The people are found.** With `people: vlm`, the default, one model call of the stage `people` is shown the camera image as it is and lists the people in it: for each a few words of appearance, a point on the body, a point on what the person faces when that is in view, and the way the person faces as seen in the image, one of eight directions or `unclear`. Depth lifts the body point to the floor. The centre of the body is taken a person's radius farther from the camera than that point, and the heading is the direction to the faced point, or else the facing turned into the world frame. An entry whose point does not lift, or lifts onto the floor, is left out. Nothing of an episode is in the request, so the episodes of a scene share one cached answer. With `people: oracle` the people are those of ground truth that are in view, with their true positions and headings, and no model is asked.
2. **They are numbered and shown.** The people found are merged (one within a person's radius of another is dropped), cut to the `max_people` nearest the target, and numbered from 1 from the left of the image. The model of the next step is shown the image with the target ringed and each number in a yellow disc on its person, the request and the subtask, what the context check wrote of the people when a model made that check, and a list with one line per person worked out from depth: how far they are from the robot and from the target, in which third of the image they are, and whom or what they face, with distances. A person who is the target is marked as such.
3. **A model states constraints.** One model call of the stage `constraints` answers with a list of constraints, each with a sentence of reason, one of six kinds, the numbers of the people it is about, a range in metres for the one kind that takes it, and whether it is about the target. At most `max_constraints` are read. The model is told what the robot does unasked, and is asked to state nothing for that. With `constraints: none` this call is not made.
4. **The solver finds the place.** It reads the floor from the depth image on a grid of `grid_resolution_m` cells in the robot's own frame: where the image shows floor, where something nearer hides it, and where something stands. Each constraint becomes a field over that floor, in metres of violation. The cells the robot can stand on and reach are compared key by key: the largest violation of any one exclusion, the sum of the exclusions' violations, the distance to the place in turn when someone is waited behind, how far behind the seen face of the target a cell lies (when the target has such a face, is no person, nobody is waited behind, and the answer does not say the robot came for a person), the sum of the preferences' violations, the distance to the standard goal, and the distance to the robot. The best cell is the place, the robot faces what a `face` constraint names (without one the last of the people it waits behind, otherwise the target), and up to `candidate_count` runners-up are kept. The solver calls no model and reads no ground truth.

Without being told, the solver keeps the robot `standoff_m` from every person found, which is that person's own space, and stops it about `standoff_m` from the target. Where the depth image shows a flat upright surface round the target point, the seen face, it also keeps the robot on the side of that face it saw: of the cells that break the exclusions least, those before the face come first, so people standing before a thing no longer send the robot round behind it. It does not read that face where the answer says the robot came for a person: a `face` that is used names somebody, and a usable `visible_to` lists that same person. The place is then chosen by that person's front, and a face fitted by depth does not rank above it. A `visible_to` alone, or one about somebody the robot is not told to face, leaves the face read. Where no usable constraint is stated, these rules and the standard goal decide alone. That is no failure, and the flag `no_usable_constraint` records it.

## The six kinds

| Kind | The place | In the solver |
|---|---|---|
| `keep_out_of_shared_space` | is outside the space between the listed people, who attend to one another. Two or more are listed. | an exclusion |
| `keep_out_of_view` | is outside what the listed people watch: towards the target with `target: true`, straight ahead of each of them otherwise. With `target: true` it is also not ahead of those of them who stand on the side of the target the robot was sent to: level with the hindmost of them, or behind, where the robot can stand on some such place without breaking another exclusion more than it does ahead of them. | an exclusion |
| `come_after` | is one taken after all the listed people, who wait their turn at the target. | an exclusion, and the place in turn |
| `within_distance` | is between `at_least` and `at_most` metres from each listed person, or from the target when nobody is listed and `target` is true. | a preference |
| `visible_to` | is where each listed person can see it without turning round. | a preference |
| `face` | is not concerned: the robot faces the listed person, the middle of several, or the target when nobody is listed and `target` is true. | the heading |

An exclusion is never traded for a preference: the place breaks no exclusion where some cell breaks none. What a kind does not read is ignored. A constraint the solver cannot use is recorded with the reason, for instance one that lists only numbers nobody has, a `within_distance` without a range, or a second `face`. The kinds and what each reads are section 6 of the design; no kind names a situation, and the solver has no branch for one.

## Run it against the baseline

Every command runs from the repository root. The first three lines make the config files: copies of `configs/default.yaml` that differ in the settings named and in nothing else.

```bash
cp configs/default.yaml constraints.yaml      # then set goal: constraints under run:
cp constraints.yaml solver_alone.yaml         # then set constraints: none under run: as well
cp constraints.yaml given_people.yaml         # then set people: oracle under run: as well
.venv/bin/python -m sgi.run --scenes data/placeholder/eval --config constraints.yaml --model mock --name constraints_plumbing
.venv/bin/python -m sgi.smoke --model gemini_fast --goal constraints --scene data/placeholder/eval/eval_08_waiting_line_4
.venv/bin/python -m sgi.run --scenes data/placeholder/eval --model gemini_fast --name baseline_gemini
.venv/bin/python -m sgi.run --scenes data/placeholder/eval --config constraints.yaml --model gemini_fast --name constraints_gemini
.venv/bin/python -m sgi.run --scenes data/placeholder/eval --config solver_alone.yaml --model gemini_fast --name solver_alone_gemini
.venv/bin/python -m sgi.run --scenes data/placeholder/eval --config given_people.yaml --model gemini_fast --name given_people_gemini
```

1. The run on `--model mock` needs no key and shows only that the path works.
2. The `sgi.smoke` line is the check to make by eye before any number of a model under this method is trusted: that the model refers to people by the numbers drawn on them. It writes the image the model was shown to `runs/smoke_numbered.png`. Run it on a few scenes with people in view.
3. The four runs then give four numbers that are read together.

| Run | Settings under `run:` | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| `baseline_gemini` | `goal: direct_marking` | The baseline: the model marks the place. |
| `constraints_gemini` | `goal: constraints` | The method. This is the run compared with the baseline: its people are found by the model, so its success and its latency come from the same calls. |
| `solver_alone_gemini` | `goal: constraints`, `constraints: none` | The solver on its own rules. What the model's constraints add is the difference between this run and the one above, and every result of the method is reported beside it. |
| `given_people_gemini` | `goal: constraints`, `people: oracle` | The constraints step with the people known exactly: its upper bound, not a result of the method. Its latency is not the method's. |

Keep the cache on and use one model entry for all four. The plan and the context check are then the same answers in every run, so all four place a goal in the same episodes, and the solver-alone run makes no call of its own: it replays the plan, the context check and the people of the run with constraints. Compare the `summary.csv` files row by row, `success_rate` and its components in the `overall` row and per `situation`. Use three trials or more for a number that is reported, and leave `--final-test` out until the end.

The lower bound of all of them is `context_check: always_no`, a robot that takes the standard goal everywhere.

## What a record shows

A result of the method in `results.jsonl` has `source: constraints` and, beside what every result has:

- `people`: the people found, each with its `number`, its floor position `xy` and heading `yaw` in the world frame, the body point `pixel`, and the `label` a model gave.
- `constraints`: one entry per constraint of the answer, in its order. The `constraint` as it was written, a `status` (`used`, `unusable`, `superseded` or `cut`), the `reason` when it was not used, and `broken_m`, by how much the place breaks it.
- `candidates`: the runners-up, each a `pose_world` with the `keys` it was ordered by.
- `solver`: the `keys` of the place, `own_space_broken_m` per person, `joined_waiting` (people the solver took as waiting though the answer did not list them), `not_ahead_of` (the people who watch the target whom the place is kept level with or behind; empty where that rule was not applied), `not_ahead_reason` (where the answer has a usable `keep_out_of_view` towards the target and that list is empty, why: a place in turn is used, the target is a person, none of the listed people stands on the side the robot was sent to, that side or the direction they watch in cannot be told, no place the robot can stand on is level with the hindmost of them or behind, or every such place breaks the other exclusions more than some place ahead of them) and `target_distance_m`. Among the keys, `face_m` is how far behind the seen face the place is: 0.0 for a place before it, positive for one that had to go behind it, null where the target has no such face, is a person, a place in turn was used, or the answer names a person to face and to be visible to.
- Six flags: `no_usable_constraint`, `constraint_unusable`, `constraints_cut`, `exclusion_broken`, `people_cut` and `far_from_target`.

With these and the scene, a record can be solved again without a model call. `calls` keeps the two calls with the answers as the model wrote them. A people call or a constraints call that brought no valid answer ends the result with that call's status and no pose, and it is scored as a failure.

When a place looks wrong, read in this order: `people` against the picture (was everyone found, and where), `constraints` (what was stated, and what was unusable), `joined_waiting`, `not_ahead_of` and `not_ahead_reason`, then the flags. `far_from_target` says the robot did not get to what it came for, whatever the cause. It is not set where the robot waited its turn or stood back with people who watch the target, however far that is; `target_distance_m` says how far. So a non-empty `not_ahead_of` together with a `target_distance_m` beyond `max_keep_m` (3 m) is the thing to check by hand: no flag is set there, and the robot may be standing back with one distant onlooker or with someone who has nothing to do with the target (Known limits). The `broken_m` of a `keep_out_of_view` with `target: true` is the larger of its zone's violation and how far ahead of the people in `not_ahead_of` the place is. `people_cut` says that more people were found than are kept, and a result with it and a `come_after` is not to be trusted, because those dropped are the farthest from the target, the end of a long line.

## What the picture shows

`viz/<scene>_<episode>.png` shows the camera image beside a top-down plot. For a result of the method both carry the number of each person found, the place and the runners-up, beside what every picture has: the target, the people of ground truth with their headings, and the acceptable and forbidden regions the scorer uses. The plot shades the floor the robot may stand on by the solver's order: the darker a cell, the earlier it comes, and the place is among the darkest. That floor is solved again from the record with the default thresholds, so for a run with other thresholds the shading can differ from what the run chose on; the place and the numbers are drawn from the record.

The regions in the picture are the scorer's. The solver never sees them.

## What is measured of a run

The scorer and the reference regions are those of the baseline: a goal of the method is scored exactly as a marked one. Six measures are added for the method. They are the last entries of a record's `measures` and columns of `summary.csv`, after every other measure and before `calls`, `cached_calls` and `cost_usd`. A column is the mean over the row's records that measured it, and it is empty where none did, which is every row of a baseline run.

| Column | What it is | Measured when |
|---|---|---|
| `people_found_share` | Of the people of ground truth in view, the share that a person found stands within twice `person_radius_m` of. Pairs are taken nearest first, and a person found counts for one person only. | `people: vlm`, and somebody is in view |
| `people_unmatched` | The number of people found that are left over: nobody of ground truth stands there, or the one who does was matched already. | `people: vlm` |
| `members_precision` | Of the numbers the answer lists under `come_after`, the share that are people waiting their turn at the target, in view. A number nobody has is listed and wrong. | the reference has people waiting at the target, a model stated the constraints, and the answer lists someone |
| `members_recall` | Of the people waiting their turn at the target, in view, the share the answer lists under `come_after`. | the reference has people waiting at the target, one of them is in view, and a model stated the constraints |
| `constraints_unusable` | The number of constraints the solver could not use: `unusable` or `superseded`. | every result the solver made |
| `exclusions_broken` | The number of exclusions the place breaks: the own space of each person it is inside, each `keep_out_of_shared_space` and `keep_out_of_view` it breaks, and `come_after` once. | every result the solver made |

The two about members are read from the answer as the model wrote it, before the solver takes anyone else as waiting. The mean of a column counts each record once, so a scene counts once for each of its episodes, and a scene with one person as much as a scene with seven. The six flags have their own columns, `flag_<name>_rate`. `sgi.rescore` carries all of these over as the run recorded them.

Latency is `latency_s` and the `latency_<stage>_s` columns, as for the baseline: the two calls of the method count under `goal`, and lifting the people and the solver under `geometry`.

## Known limits

These are stated in the design (its items B6 and B7) and are deliberately left in: it was decided on 9 October 2026 that no further rule goes into the solver before it has been built and measured. In most of them the place is wrong and no flag says so. They are what to look for when results are read. The cases with a test are pinned in `tests/test_solver.py`, most of them under "the limits that are stated, and stay"; of the limits of the seen side, the design's B6 says which are pinned elsewhere in that file, and where.

**Who is last in a line is read from distance alone.** The last of the waiting people is the one farthest from the marked target point, and no heading is read for it. So a line is ordered rightly only where its end is farther from the mark than its head.

- A line along the front of a wide thing, with the mark at its middle, is ended at its head: the robot is sent a metre beyond the person who is being served. With three people 0.8 m apart the test has it 2.6 m from the person who is really last.
- A line that turns back towards the target is ordered wrongly in the same way.
- A single line that turns a corner with one person past it is ended beside its end, not behind it, because its last two people are equally far from the mark. The robot stands 1.0 to 1.5 m from the person who is really last, beside them.
- The scorer cannot show the first two. Its reference rule for a waiting line orders by distance from the target as well, so in such a scene it would call the wrong place right and the right place wrong. Such a scene needs regions drawn by the exporter.

**Whoever stands behind the end of a line is taken as waiting.** A person the answer does not list, who stands behind the last waiting person and within `standoff_m` of the place in turn, joins the line as its new last, and the test is repeated from them. This is what makes an answer that leaves out the last person harmless. It also takes a bystander, and a chain of them: with three passers-by one behind another behind a line of four, all three are taken and the robot stands about 4.3 m behind the last person who waits. `joined_waiting` names those taken; no flag is set. People the answer lists as a shared space are not taken. People it lists under a view are.

- The other side of it: a waiting person left out of the answer who stands too far behind the others is not taken, and the robot stands in the gap, ahead of them. In the tests the person is taken with 0.6 to 1.9 m between people and not with 2.2 m.
- An unrelated person listed under `come_after`, farther from the target than the rest, becomes the end of the line.
- Two people who talk while they wait, given as a shared space and left out of `come_after`, are not taken as waiting, and the robot stands beside the line, ahead of them.

**People who wait in a loose arc.** The place is behind one of those farthest from the target, and which one can change with centimetres.

**People who watch the target.** With a `keep_out_of_view` towards the target the robot stands level with the hindmost of the listed people who are on the side of the target it was sent to, or behind them. That side is before the seen face, and for a target without one the side the standard goal stands on. Neither this rule nor the seen face acts where the answer states `come_after` for people who watch, where the place in turn is kept, nor where the target is a person. The counts below are of the 90 recorded runs that hold solver steps, solved again from their records (4344 records, 2295 solver steps; see "What has and has not been measured").

- **The hindmost listed person decides, with no bound.** An unrelated person listed with a row of watchers, 2.7 m behind it, keeps the robot back with them. In the test, seen from behind them all, the robot stands level with that person, 1.2 m beside them and about 5.6 m from the thing. `far_from_target` is not set where people are joined, so only `not_ahead_of` and `target_distance_m` show it.
- **Where the robot can stand on no place level with that person or behind, nobody is joined.** The rule cannot be met there, so it is not applied at all, to the row no more than to the person behind it. In the test the same four are seen from the side and the picture shows no floor level with the person at the back: the robot stands about 2.0 m from the thing, ahead of the row, inside nobody's own space and with no flag, and `not_ahead_reason` names the person. `far_from_target` is judged there as without the rule. In the recorded runs this is 4 of the 72 steps on the scenes of people watching a thing, each with people the model had placed on the far wall; all four failed before and fail now.
- **A sliver of floor.** Where floor level with the hindmost person is in view but all of it lies within `standoff_m` of them, nobody is joined either. The rule is applied only where the robot can stand level with them or behind without breaking another exclusion more than it does ahead of them; so where some place breaks no exclusion, the rule never puts the robot inside a person's own space. In the test, with one person 4 m along a wall from a mark on it, the robot stands a metre before the mark, out of their view and more than 4 m ahead of them, with nothing broken and no flag, and `not_ahead_reason` says why. Until the ruling of 11 October 2026 on this case the rule was applied there, and the robot stood 0.14 m inside their own space. None of the 63 recorded steps that join people breaks an exclusion, and none of the 2295 recorded steps has this reason.
- **One person is enough.** The rule reads every view of the target as people to join. It does not look at how many they are, how far off they stand, or whether the answer also asks to be near the target. In the test one onlooker stands 4 m before a thing: the robot stands 1.1 m beside them and 4.2 m from the thing, where unasked it stops a metre before it. Nothing is flagged, and a `within_distance` about the target changes nothing, because it is a preference. Outside the scenes of people watching a thing, 14 of 2223 recorded steps join people, 10 of them more than 3 m from the target; 11 of the 14 failed before and fail now.
- **A false person on the watched thing.** When the people step puts a person on the target itself, that person is taken for the target person. Neither rule is then applied, and the robot goes round behind the thing as it did before the rules were added. The record shows it by a null `face_m` and by `not_ahead_reason`. This is 3 of the 36 recorded steps of one model on the scenes of people watching a thing.
- **A target person the people step missed.** A person the robot needs whom the list of people lacks, or has more than a body's radius from where they stand, is a thing to the solver. The seen face can then be fitted on their body. It is read unless the answer names that person to face and to be visible to; read whatever the answer said, it cost 3 recorded successes of conversation groups, which that exception restores. With `visible_to` of them and no `face` of them it is read: in the test the robot then stays beside the person, at the plane of that face, and does not come round to where they look. The rule about people who watch is applied as to any thing: in the test, of four in a circle with the one needed listed 0.67 m off, the three others are joined and the robot stands across the circle from the one it needs, 1.9 m from the point marked on them. In the recorded runs it acts in 6 steps of conversation groups, where it loses 1 success and gains 1.
- **A person behind a thing, named to face and to be visible to.** That exception reads the answer, not the target. Where the target is a real thing and the answer names a person behind it to face and to be visible to, the seen face is not read, and the robot can go behind the thing. In the test someone stands behind a low thing and looks away from the robot: the robot goes round, 0.85 m behind the face and about 1.7 m from the mark, with no flag and a null `face_m`. Where that person looks at the robot over the thing, the same answer leaves the robot before the face, with nobody else before the thing. The exception acts wherever the named person stands: with three people talking before a thing that stands free, their shared space stated, the place to be visible to the first and `face` 1, the robot goes 0.94 m behind the face (1.08 m from the mark), where the thing hides it from them. In the recorded runs the two are stated together, where the face would otherwise be read, in 24 steps, 16 of them on a target that ground truth has as a thing.
- **The mark on a narrow side.** A robot at the flank of a watched thing gets its target point on the thing's narrow side face. The people before the true front are then joined only where their centre lies before the plane of that side face. In the test 4 cm decide: with the nearest of a row 2 cm before that plane the robot stands behind the row, 4.7 m from the mark, and with them 2 cm behind it the robot stays a metre before the side face.
- People who watch the target and are stated with `target: false` are not joined. In the test the robot stands about 2.1 m from the thing, more than a metre ahead of the row.
- Where the answer states `come_after` for people who watch, the place in turn is kept, and neither rule of the seen side acts.
- A thing without a seen face, such as a low one marked on its top, takes its side from the standard goal, which follows the robot's own line of approach. With three people on one side of such a thing and two on the other, and the robot coming along the gap between the two sides, one person of each side is joined and the robot stops about 1.77 m from the thing, nearer than any of the five.
- The seen face orders the places and removes none. Where every place before the face breaks more of an exclusion than some place behind it, the robot still goes behind, and `face_m` says how far.
- The fit takes the near side of a round post of 0.2 m radius for a seen face. Its front then points at the camera, wherever the robot stands.
- Which end of a symmetric row the robot stands at is a near-tie, which the standard goal settles to the millimetre. In the test, at hand-picked places and on no picture, 2 mm of the standard goal decide it, whichever side the robot comes from.

**One distance for everyone, and groups.** Own space is `standoff_m` round every person found, the neighbours of the person the robot needs among them. People in a group stand closer to one another than that leaves room for, so `visible_to` loses to own space: the robot stands at arm's length beside the person it needs and well round from where they look. For six, eight and ten people in a circle the tests have it at least 110, 120 and 125 degrees off the facing of the one it needs, behind their shoulder, with no flag set. The space between people adds to this at any spacing: six people 2 m apart leave the robot 105 to 120 degrees off as well. The design expects `visible_to` not to be met in any group of three or more.

**A crowd keeps the robot out.** Where people stand closer together than 1.41 m, which is the square root of 2 times `standoff_m`, no cell among them is free of every own space, and a cell that breaks no exclusion beats every cell that breaks one. The robot then stays outside, metres from the person or thing it needs. In the test, with the person needed in the middle of 25 people 1.4 m apart or less, it stops 3.2 to 3.7 m away with no exclusion broken. `far_from_target` is set there. It is set only beyond `max_keep_m`, so a somewhat tighter crowd can leave the robot just inside that distance with nothing flagged.

**Near-ties.** Two places that are equally good to within a cell are told apart by where the grid's cells fall, and the grid lies in the robot's frame. So the place can go from one side of a person to the other when the robot itself stands a few centimetres elsewhere. Both places break no exclusion and are the same in preference; either is right. With four or more people listed as one shared space, which pairs are neighbours can also turn on centimetres.

**Smaller ones.** When the people posing for a picture are listed under `keep_out_of_view` together with the one who takes it, the floor behind those posing is not excluded; listing the one who takes it alone excludes it. A `within_distance` of nearly zero about a thing is obeyed only as far as the floor allows, and about a person own space keeps the robot at `standoff_m`. The floor is what one depth image shows: floor the image hides can be crossed and not stood on.

**What the method does not have.** Worked examples for the constraints step. Reference constraints for a picture being taken and for people waiting at a lift: each of the two has a reference rule ([`scene_format.md`](scene_format.md), section 7) and placeholder scenes, and so a score, but nobody has written down the constraints a careful answer holds there. A person detector other than the model. Moving people.

## What has and has not been measured

**Measured, by the tests, with no model.** The suite runs the solver on hand-made layouts, field by field and key by key, and on the placeholder scenes. One test is the design's check of the solver with reference constraints, `tests/test_reference_constraints.py`: for every placeholder scene of nobody at the target, of a waiting line, of a conversation group and of people watching a screen it writes the constraints a careful answer would hold from who is in the group at the target, and for a scene of another situation it gives none; it takes the people from ground truth, and asserts the solver's own properties. The place breaks no exclusion where some cell breaks none, no cell the robot could stand on comes before it in the order, and no such cell lies outside the scene's static free space. Where people watch a screen it also asserts that the view is not broken, which is the solver's own figure, and measures one thing from the place itself, without the solver's word and without the scorer: that the robot stands level with the hindmost of them or behind. It then prints what the scorer says of those places, per situation, as a count with no bar to meet:

```bash
.venv/bin/pytest tests/test_reference_constraints.py -rP
```

That count is not a result of the method. No model is in it, and for a waiting line it is not evidence for the rule either: the place in turn is built as the reference region for a waiting line is built, from the last person by distance, so with the right members it lies inside that region by construction. Nor is it evidence where people watch a screen: standing no nearer the watched thing than those who watch it is, by the report of the rule's designer, the shape of the reference rule there as well (the design, section 3).

**Measured on recorded runs, with no model.** A record of a run of the method holds what the solver was given: the target point, the people found and the constraints as the model wrote them. A replay tool, kept with the development notes and not part of this repository, solves every such record again with the solver as it is now, on the scene the run used, and scores the new place. Run over every recorded run that holds solver steps, it is the regression gate for a change to the solver.

It prints, per situation and in all, the successes as recorded and as replayed, how many records gained and lost theirs, and every record that lost it. It fails when a record lost it that is not on its list of accepted losses; that list holds the one loss that is known and stated. The list names 90 runs with 4344 records and 2295 solver steps; the runs and their scenes are local and not in git.
