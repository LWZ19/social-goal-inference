## 1. Goals as distributions, heatmaps or candidate sets

**Summary (not checked):** The newest directly relevant work treats the stopping place as a map or distribution, but I found no paper that evaluates sets or calibration for social goal poses. "Where Should I Join?" (arXiv:2609.28467) is the main precedent. It predicts an energy map over feasible floor cells plus cos/sin heading maps, argues joining behaviour is "inherently multimodal", takes the arg-min cell as the goal, and scores 10 sampled poses per scenario (averaged, not best-of-k). Its human study scores the distribution by KL divergence to human choices. Dual-Heatmap grounding (2605.19420) and RoboMAP (2510.10912) move VLM goals from a point to a heatmap. In both, the evaluation still reduces the map to its arg-max point. Dual-Heatmap is the only one that consumes the map directly, through an MPPI planner. RoboMAP's authors say downstream policies underuse the heatmap. VoxPoser (2307.05973) is the template for composable affordance and avoidance maps. Its weighted sum (affordance x2, avoidance x1) is a scalar cousin of this project's lexicographic solver. Its stated main error source is perception, the same weakness seen here in people detection. VLFM (2312.03275) shows that confidence-weighted fusion of value maps helps. For uncertainty, KnowNo (2307.01928) and ENCP (2609.17499) give conformal set construction with finite-sample coverage. Both work over finite option lists, which fits the solver's ranked candidates. Neither is applied to continuous goal poses. Nav2 NavigateThroughPoses visits poses in order, so it does not accept alternative goals. A candidate set therefore has to be reduced to one pose, or to a goal region or cost map, before it reaches Nav2. Implication: the solver's cell scores or runner-up places can be exposed as a goal set or map, and best-of-k, mass-in-polygon, and conformal coverage can be reported offline from recorded runs. The headroom measured here (any of 5 vs the top place) is the same quantity as best-of-k. A learned energy head is a possible add-on. It would need its own training and held-out design.

**Surprises (not checked):** In 'Where Should I Join?', the 'distribution' is evaluated by sampling 10 poses per scenario (matching 10 VLM trials) and scoring validity, plus a KL divergence to human choices. It does not use best-of-k or coverage. The offline comparison is therefore about expected validity, not set headroom. / The two 2026 heatmap papers both reduce the map to its arg-max point when scoring (RoboMAP says so explicitly, calling it overly simplistic). Only Dual-Heatmap uses the map directly, in an MPPI planner, and it reports no ablation of arg-max vs full map. / Nav2's NavigateThroughPoses takes an ordered list of waypoints, not alternatives, so a ranked candidate set cannot go straight into the standard multi-goal action. / Plain conformal prediction undercovered in every reported ENCP entry (e.g. 0.854 vs a 0.90 target) when decisions within an episode depend on each other, and its guarantee does not extend to seen-to-unseen shift. This matters for calibrating on three scene sets and using the result on held-out situations. / A hand-written rule baseline (64.29% pose validity) beats prompted GPT-5.5 and Gemini 3 Flash (about 51-60%) in the group-joining paper's oracle-group table, and a trained model beats both. This is a comparison point for the project's rule-like solver, though the benchmarks differ.

**Open questions (not checked):** I found no paper that reports conformal prediction sets, best-of-k, mass-in-valid-region or calibration for social goal poses (where to stand). The evaluation protocols for sets are borrowed from other areas (KnowNo, ENCP). / Conformal methods found are for finite option lists (actions, plans). I did not find a conformal method for a continuous goal region on the floor, with coverage measured as 'valid polygon hit'. A calibrated top-k over the solver's 5 cm cells or runner-up places looks open. / No paper found that compares a VLM-plus-geometric-solver goal set against a learned energy or heatmap head on the same social scenes, or that measures a single arg-max point against a set from the same model. / Not found: any 2025-2026 work citing 2609.28467 (the paper is only weeks old), and any work on goal distributions for people watching a screen or for queue ends. / Not checked: Nav2 goal-checker behaviour with multiple goals, and goal-region planning (sampling planners with goal regions). Whether a 'goal region' (polygon) can be given to Nav2 without a custom plugin is unverified. / I did not fetch the full text of 2602.09002 beyond the abstract page, so its candidate-path scoring details and numbers are unverified. I also did not find the ENCP author list.

- **Where Should I Join? Robot Group Joining via Language-Guided Goal Prediction** — 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28467
  - Relevance: Closest prior work and the clearest instance of a distribution-valued social goal (energy map plus heading maps). It supplies validity thresholds and baseline numbers to compare against, and its evaluation shows a sampling protocol that the project could copy. The pitfall for comparison is that its benchmark is real-image (JRDB, SCAND) with 42 scenarios, so numbers are not directly comparable.
  - [verified] The goal predictor outputs an energy map E(x,y) over feasible floor cells plus cos/sin orientation maps. The goal is the minimum-energy cell, and the heading is read at that cell.
  - [verified] Offline pose validity on 42 scenarios (conversation, queue, audience): ours 89.76% (position 90.47, orientation 96.67; 1.108 s) vs GPT-5.5 few-shot 59.76% (49.25 s), Gemini 3 Flash few-shot 53.57% (27.80 s), Gemini 3 Flash zero-shot 51.19%, Molmo2 10.71%. With oracle groups: ours 94.76%, handcrafted rule 64.29%, multivariate regression 35.71%.
  - [verified] Distributional evaluation: 10 goal poses are sampled per scenario to match the VLM trials, and the pilot human study uses KL divergence to 15 participants' choices (ours 14.87+/-1.08, Gemini 3 Flash 17.75+/-3.61). In real-robot goal updating, the goal is kept if it lies within 0.4 m of any of the top-5 new candidates.
  - [verified] The failure analysis of five low-validity scenes attributes failures to grounding errors and overly conservative joining distance. The environment is used mainly as a feasibility constraint, not as a source of joining affordances. Drop 'mainly audience scenes' unless it is verified in the PDF.
- **Beyond Waypoints: Dual-Heatmap Grounding for Cross-Embodiment Semantic Navigation** — Kaijie Yun, Yue Chen, 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19420
  - Relevance: A recent example of a VLM-conditioned dense floor heatmap (where to stand) plus a facing heatmap, consumed directly by a sampling planner. It matches the idea of a goal-plus-heading map and shows the failure of an arg-max reduction. The weak setup (simulation only, narrow baselines) means the numbers are suggestive only.
  - [verified] Qwen3-VL (LoRA) with a conv decoder gives separate nav and facing heatmaps, with labels from depth-projected distance fields and SAM3 masks. About 130 Isaac Sim scenes were imported, and the full training corpus is 345,906 samples. That corpus includes other sources, and the paper's own generated portion is about 40.8k samples.
  - [verified] For evaluation, the point is the heatmap maximum if confidence exceeds 0.5, otherwise no prediction. For execution, an MPPI planner scores trajectories by integrating alpha*M_nav + beta*D_fac along the path, with no single goal point chosen.
  - [verified] On the MP3D val_test split (Table 1) the Affordance Rate (predicted point lies in a reachable region) is 0.7265 for ours vs 0.2492 Molmo, 0.2138 Qwen3-VL-Instruct and 0.0622 InternVLA-N1-System2. Simulation success on unseen scenes: Jetbot 0.6517 vs 0.3686 for a fine-tuned Qwen3-VL, H1 0.5693 vs 0.2200, Aliengo 0.4790 vs 0.1548.
  - [verified] Caveats: simulation only, no real robot, no seeds or variance, three 8B baselines, and Section 4 and Table 1 disagree on whether the comparison is zero-shot.
- **More than A Point: Capturing Uncertainty with Adaptive Affordance Heatmaps for Spatial Grounding in Robotic Tasks (RoboMAP)** — Xinyu Shao, Yanzhe Tang, Pengwei Xie, Kaiwen Zhou, Yuzheng Zhuang, Xingyue Quan, Jianye Hao, Long Zeng, Xiu Li, 2025, CoRL 2026 poster (per the project GitHub page). https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.10912
  - Relevance: Argues that a heatmap carries grounding uncertainty a point cannot, which is the proposal's 'distribution over poses' idea. Its own limitation, that results use only the top point and downstream policies underuse the map, warns the project to define how the set is consumed and scored.
  - [verified] A PaliGemma encoder with an Adaptive Heatmap Decoder predicts a coarse map plus a per-pixel k x k kernel. Targets are Gaussians synthesised from sparse point, box or trajectory labels, with BCE loss. Inference takes 0.04 s (Embodied-R1: 2.18 s).
  - [verified] Benchmarks are point-based, so only the highest-confidence point is extracted for evaluation. The authors call the single peak-pixel heuristic 'overly simplistic' and say the heatmap information is 'underutilized by downstream policies'.
  - [verified] Navigation is shown zero-shot in simulated, indoor and outdoor scenes against RoboBrain2.0-7B and BridgeVLA, qualitatively only, with no quantitative navigation metrics.
  - [verified] Results: Where2place 73.00% (Embodied-R1 69.50%), RoboRefIt 88.73%, RefSpatial 36.50% (Embodied-R1 38.46%, so it does not win there), real-world manipulation 82% (10 trials per task).
- **VoxPoser: Composable 3D Value Maps for Robotic Manipulation with Language Models** — Wenlong Huang et al., 2023, CoRL 2023. https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.05973
  - Relevance: The template for language-composed affordance and avoidance maps. The weighted sum of the two maps is the scalar analogue of this project's constraint solver. It supports trying a soft map output and shows the reliance on perception.
  - [verified] GPT-4 writes code that builds 3D voxel maps (affordance, avoidance, plus velocity, rotation and gripper maps). Affordance maps get a Euclidean distance transform and avoidance maps a Gaussian filter. The planner cost is the negative weighted sum of normalised affordance (weight 2) and avoidance (weight 1) maps, replanned at 5 Hz.
  - [partly] Real-world (Table 1, 5 tasks, 10 trials each per condition): VoxPoser 88.0% static and 70.0% with disturbances, vs LLM+Primitives 24.0% static and 0.0% with disturbances.
  - [partly] Limitations (Section 5): relies on external perception modules; needs a general-purpose dynamics model for contact-rich tasks; the planner considers only end-effector trajectories; LLM prompts need manual engineering. Section 4.4 says most real-world errors are from perception.
- **Robots That Ask For Help: Uncertainty Alignment for Large Language Model Planners (KnowNo)** — Allen Z. Ren et al., 2023, CoRL 2023. https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01928
  - Relevance: The standard recipe for a conformal prediction set over a finite option list from an LLM, with coverage guarantee and ask-for-help rule. It maps to a set of solver candidate places (a finite list), though the guarantee assumes the scene is fully grounded, which the project's perception errors violate.
  - [partly] Nonconformity score is 1 minus the LLM confidence of the true option. q-hat is the ceil((N+1)(1-eps))/N empirical quantile, and the set holds options with confidence >= 1-q-hat. Eq. 1 coverage is marginal. In practice KnowNo uses the dataset-conditional guarantee (Eq. 2) with N=400 and delta=0.01, adjusting epsilon-hat so coverage 1-eps holds with probability 0.99 over the calibration set. The robot acts if the set is a singleton and asks otherwise.
  - [verified] Table 1 (50 hardware trials): KnowNo task success 0.74, set size 1.72, help 0.92 per trial (0.58 per step) vs Simple Set 0.72, 2.04, 1.00 per trial (0.72 per step). Plan success was matched using eps=0.25 vs 0.42. Table 2 (PaLM-2L): task success 0.76, set size 2.22, help 0.67 vs 0.75, 2.38, 0.81, with eps=0.15 vs 0.24.
  - [verified] The guarantee assumes objects are fully grounded in the text given to the LLM and that proposed actions execute successfully. Perception and low-level action uncertainty are not in the calibration.
- **ENCP: Episode-Normalized Conformal Prediction for Vision-and-Language Navigation** — authors not retrieved, 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17499
  - Relevance: A current conformal result for navigation sets: it shows that naive per-step conformal prediction undercovers when decisions are dependent, and that the guarantee fails under shift. This is a warning for calibrating on the project's three scene sets and applying it to held-out situations.
  - [partly] Plain CP undercovers on R2R val-unseen at alpha=0.10 (target 0.90; DUET 0.854, HAMT 0.883, THR score). ENCP reaches 0.943 (HAMT) to 0.965 (DUET), with mean set sizes of 3.8 (HAMT) to 6.7 (DUET).
  - [verified] The guarantee requires exchangeable calibration and test episodes and does not cover seen-to-unseen shift. ENCP assumes a finite action space. (The continuous-navigation point is an inference, not confirmed as stated by the paper.)
  - [verified] Simulated help-seeking (DUET, R2R): no help gives 71.2% success. Asking when the set exceeds 15 actions gives 76.9% at a 6.4% ask rate; a threshold of 8 gives 91.1% at 32.3%. The assistant is an oracle and the threshold was swept on val-unseen.
- **VLFM: Vision-Language Frontier Maps for Zero-Shot Semantic Navigation** — Naoki Yokoyama, Sehoon Ha, Dhruv Batra, Jiuguang Wang, Bernadette Bucher, 2023, ICRA 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.03275
  - Relevance: The reference value-map-over-floor method for goal selection with a probability-like map, with a measured benefit from confidence-weighted fusion over views. Relevant if the project ever fuses several VLM answers or samples into one floor map.
  - [verified] The value map has a semantic score channel (BLIP-2 cosine similarity to 'Seems like there is a <target_object> ahead') and a confidence channel that falls as cos^2 from the optical axis. Repeated observations fuse by confidence-weighted average, and the highest-value frontier is chosen.
  - [verified] Update-rule ablation (SPL / SR): replacement 26.5/44.5 on HM3D, unweighted average 30.0/51.8, confidence-weighted 30.4/52.5. Gibson 52.2/84.0 and MP3D 17.5/36.4 with the full method.
  - [partly] The single-floor restriction is stated in Section VI-A (failure analysis), not the Limitations paragraph: VLFM currently supports only single-floor episodes because odometry lacks a z coordinate (stair traversal accounts for 14.6% HM3D and 9.6% MP3D failures). The Limitations text (Sec. VII) says targets are assumed easily visible from the default camera height, and the map provides only task-specific semantic information, so it cannot be reused for sequential searches for different objects or for language-specified tasks such as vision-language navigation.
- **NavigateThroughPoses Action (Nav2)** — Nav2 project, 2026. https://api.nav2.org/actions/rolling/navigatethroughposes.html
  - Relevance: Shows how a standard planner consumes goal lists. The action takes an ordered waypoint list, not a set of alternatives, so a candidate-set output has to be reduced to one pose or a cost map before execution (unless a custom behaviour tree is written).
  - [verified] The goal is an array of poses (nav_msgs/Goals) and an optional behavior_tree path. The action is described as 'Navigate robot through a sequence of poses in order'.
  - [verified] Feedback includes number_of_poses_remaining, distance_remaining, estimated_time_remaining and number_of_recoveries. The result has per-waypoint waypoint_statuses and an error_code.

## 2. LLM/VLM to constraints, cost maps and value functions for social navigation

**Summary (not checked):** The VLM-to-cost literature (2024-2026) is almost entirely about path and local-motion social navigation, not about choosing a stopping pose from one image. In that literature the VLM does one of four jobs: score candidate actions (VLM-Social-Nav), label people so a planner avoids groups (GSON), select among geometrically generated candidate paths (From Obstacles to Etiquette), or assign per-object social scores that become Gaussian cost layers (G2-Nav, LISN). A 2026 survey (arXiv 2606.28760) groups these as a "bridge" from VLM semantics to goals, cost weights and hard constraints. It lists "reasoning-to-control grounding" as underdeveloped. It does not discuss queues, F-formations or pose selection.

Three patterns matter for this project. (1) Calling the VLM only for a symbolic or perceptual output and handing the result to a deterministic geometric stage is the common design. Our constraint method fits it. No one in this set has a VLM write general spatial constraints for a social placement problem. ReKep does this for manipulation, and its optimizer causes few failures. The VLM and the point tracker do. (2) Latency is the open issue everyone reports. Papers use YOLO triggers, smaller models, distillation or asynchronous loops, and report about 1-9 s per VLM call. Our 30-60 s per episode is far outside this range. The mentor's own group-joining paper reports 28-49 s for prompted frontier models against 1.1 s for a trained model. (3) Classical proxemics and F-formation placement is old and solved by geometry. The o-space circle fit with personal-space and free-space terms gives poses in the middle of a conversation group, not at its edge. Queue goals are classically hand-set or geometric. A VLM-to-constraints solver for queue, group and audience from one image, scored by a polygon, appears to be unexplored. Using one frozen VLM with a distribution output, as the proposal permits, is also unexplored.

**Surprises (not checked):** The mentor's paper reports no latency measurements for its fine-tuned 7B VLM, only 'significantly lower latency' than GPT-4o, although it presents distillation as the route to real time. Latency is argued, not measured. / Under the mentor's own metrics, VLM-Social-Nav and ViNT time out in the queuing scenario (NT 90 s, failed), so a VLM-as-scorer is weak where a goal depends on the line's structure. / G2-Nav's ablation without the safeguard has a better L2 (0.48) than the full model (0.55), and without verification it is almost the same (0.57), so the main benefit comes from VLM scoring alone (1.12 without it). / GSON's binary group label (avoid all groups) cannot separate a queue from a chat, which its own limitation section says. The distinction our constraints draw by kind (wait behind, keep out of space) is the fix its authors name but do not build. / Prompted frontier models score 47-60% pose validity at 28-49 s per query in the mentor's group-joining paper, against 90% in 1.1 s for a trained model. Our constraint method sits in this same range of success but is not faster. / VLM-Social-Nav's headline gains (27.38% success, 19.05% collision) are percentage-point differences against DWA, not relative improvements, and the earlier version listed larger numbers. / ReKep's failure analysis says the optimizer causes little error and the VLM and the tracker cause most of it, the same split our offline data shows with oracle people.

**Open questions (not checked):** No work found that has a VLM propose general spatial constraints (keep out, line up behind, stay visible) and a deterministic solver pick a stop pose for social situations. ReKep does it for manipulation, and the social papers use fixed cost vocabularies per object. This looks open, but it rests on a limited search (CoNVOI, OLiVia-Nav and Social-LLaVA results were not read in full). / No comparison of explicit geometric solvers (o-space fit, queue-line extension) against VLM-marked points for the same stop-pose task. The mentor's paper compares only with a handcrafted rule and regression. / How large the gap is between the VLM writing constraints and a hand-written reference constraint set (the analogue of ReKep's 44.3% auto against 68.6% annotated). Our oracle-people result suggests a gap but no oracle-constraint run exists. / No latency study of constraint generation versus direct pointing. The cheapest fix seen elsewhere is a shorter structured output (G2-Nav: a few numbers per object, 1.5 s with a 2B model). Whether a smaller output schema keeps accuracy with Qwen, GLM and DeepSeek is untested. / Queue joining by robots is mostly hand-set or geometric goals (PaCcET line fit, straight queues). A queue-end detector robust to occlusion, bystanders and curved lines was not found in the literature. / Set or distribution outputs from a constraint solver (feasible-region mass, runner-up poses) are not reported anywhere in social navigation; only learned energy maps (the mentor's paper) and sampled geometric methods (Vazquez et al.) give distributions. / Not read in full: OLiVia-Nav (arXiv 2409.13675), Social-LLaVA (2501.09024), Vi-LAD (2503.09820), Narrate2Nav, CoNVOI (no search hit), NaviWM (2510.23509, fetch failed), the 2026 survey beyond its first 100k characters, and HUMA (2607.10991, abstract only).

- **From Obstacles to Etiquette: Robot Social Navigation with VLM-Informed Path Selection** — 2026, RA-L 2026 (per project brief). https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09002
  - Relevance: The mentor's paper. It is the select-among-candidates design, in contrast to our solve-from-constraints design. It reports no latency numbers, so our latency gap cannot be compared to it directly. Its stated limits (no memory of long queues, confounding activities) are our target cases.
  - [verified] Candidate paths come from a classical pipeline (YOLOv10, ByteTrack, a human trajectory predictor, A* through Poisson-disk anchors, Hausdorff clustering to 3-6 paths). A VLM sees the paths drawn on the image and outputs a one-sentence description per path, a chosen path number and a justification.
  - [verified] Distillation precedent: GPT-4o labels 4,851 SCAND image-selection pairs (574 validation) and Qwen-2.5 7B is fully fine-tuned, served with vLLM at 4-bit on an H100. The paper gives no measured latency, only 'significantly lower latency' than GPT-4o.
  - [partly] On a Spot robot in four scenarios (3 trials each) it has SIT 0.00 and Max SIR 0% in all four, tied with some baselines in queuing. There VLM-Social-Nav and ViNT fail (NT set to 90 s) and GSON has PSV 0.38 vs 0.33 for ours, but VLM-Social-Nav has PSV 0.00. Separately, an offline ablation on 10 scenes (50 trials each) shows group interruption rising from 8.89% at 2.4 s to 39.17% at 0 s.
  - [verified] Limitations (Sec. VI): the VLM sees only the current frame, so it misses context beyond the view such as long queues. Simultaneous or ambiguous activities confuse its prioritisation. Post-processing may remove the ideal candidate. Separately, Sec. III-A assumes low to moderate crowd density.
- **G2-Nav: Grounded and Guarded Vision-Language Costmaps for Robot Social Navigation** — Yuwen Liao, Yihang Lan, Yizhuo Yang, Ruimeng Liu, Xinhang Xu, Shenghai Yuan, Lihua Xie, 2026, CoRL 2026 (listed on arXiv page). https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16956
  - Relevance: The newest VLM-to-costmap work and a close cousin of the constraint idea. The VLM emits a few numbers per object and geometry does the rest. It shows small VLMs give 1.5-4 s latency when output is short, and it names the failure we should expect (uniform scores). It evaluates path following, not stop poses.
  - [verified] Latency: Qwen3.5-2B on two RTX 2080 GPUs averages 1.5 s per query (recorded setting). GPT-5.4-Nano averages 4 s (interactive setting). Stale scores at 4 s latency keep 82% of the L2 and 81% of the MAOE benefit.
  - [partly] Recorded setting, averaged over 83 trials: G2-Nav 0.55 m L2 / 15.64 deg MAOE against 0.97 / 20.14 for path-etiquette. Without VLM scoring it is 1.12 / 21.08, which is worse than path-etiquette on both metrics. In the interactive setting (6 trials per method) G2-Nav had 0 safety and 0 boundary interventions. Drop the 'largest ablation drop' wording unless the other ablation rows are checked.
  - [verified] Limitation: the VLM may hallucinate by 'assigning the same score to all objects, leading to over-conservative behaviours', and the safeguard only checks unregistered nearby point clouds.
- **VLM-Social-Nav: Socially Aware Robot Navigation through Scoring using Vision-Language Models** — Daeun Song, Jing Liang, Amirreza Payandeh, Amir Hossain Raj, Xuesu Xiao, Dinesh Manocha, 2025, IEEE RA-L, 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.00210
  - Relevance: The baseline for the VLM-as-scorer design. The VLM's output is reduced to a heading and speed term in a DWA cost. It never sees a stop-pose problem, and it fails in the queue scenario of the mentor's paper (timeout). Its detector trigger is a standard way to cut VLM latency.
  - [verified] Social cost C_social = w_l*|v - v_h| + w_a*|w - w_h|, where v_h and w_h come from a zero-shot GPT-4V answer in the fixed format 'Move DIRECTION with SPEED'. The total cost C = alpha*C_goal + beta*C_obst + gamma*C_social is minimised by DWA, and the VLM is queried for each candidate action.
  - [verified] Average GPT-4V inference time is 'around three seconds' (Sec. IV-A). Sec. IV-E says large VLMs need 'several seconds'. No 2-3 s range is stated.
  - [verified] Table I (21 runs each): success 100% in frontal, gesture, intersection and doorway scenarios against DWA at 100/0/90.48/100. Collision 14.29/0/4.76/9.52% against DWA at 28.57/19.05/19.05/38.10%. The abstract's 27.38% and 19.05% are percentage-point differences, not relative gains.
  - [verified] Limits: future work is needed for complex scenarios with multiple individuals, since simple left/right directions may not suffice with multiple groups. Each scenario had seven user-study participants. The paper says 'relying solely on VLMs for navigation decisions is not safe'. Experiments were indoor only, and large VLMs need several seconds per response.
- **GSON: A Group-based Social Navigation Framework with Large Multimodal Model** — Shangyi Luo et al. (Tsinghua Shenzhen, NUS), 2024, arXiv (venue not checked). https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.18084
  - Relevance: The closest group-aware precedent. The LMM only labels groups, and geometry (convex hull, ellipse, replanned segment) does the rest. Its grouping accuracy numbers by model size are a useful reference for our people-perception stage. Its binary group label cannot tell a queue from a chat, which is the gap our constraints could fill.
  - [verified] Pipeline: a LiDAR detector plus YOLO-V5 and a Kalman tracker give people. An annotated keyframe (boxes, masks, IDs) goes to gpt-4o-2024-08-06, which returns groups. The planner fits an ellipse to each group's convex hull as a social space and replans only the path segment crossing it with BIT*. Local control uses NMPC with control barrier functions.
  - [verified] Grouping accuracy: GPT-4o 77.8% accurate (8.8% miss), Qwen2.5-VL-7B 53.6% (14.8% miss), DBSCAN only 10.8-14.8% accurate with 41.9-46.5% extra groups.
  - [verified] Latency: about 8 s for Gemini 2.5 Pro and Claude 3.7 Sonnet, about 4 s for GPT-4o and Gemini 2.0 Flash, about 2 s for Qwen2.5-VL-32B, about 1 s for Qwen2.5-VL-7B (Sec. V-A). V-D says 1.5-2 s typical with the 32B model, while the footnote names GPT-4o as the grouping model, so the deployed model is ambiguous.
  - [partly] Do not present the quote as verbatim. The paper says traversing a queue may be more acceptable than interrupting a group chat, and that its binary group labels make the planner conservatively avoid all groups.
- **ReKep: Spatio-Temporal Reasoning of Relational Keypoint Constraints for Robotic Manipulation** — Wenlong Huang, Chen Wang, Yunzhu Li, Ruohan Zhang, Li Fei-Fei, 2024, CoRL 2024 (not checked on the page). https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01652
  - Relevance: The best-documented VLM-writes-constraints, optimizer-solves design. Its failure analysis says the optimizer contributes little and the VLM-referred keypoints cause errors. That supports our choice of a deterministic solver and tells us to measure which constraint errors the VLM makes.
  - [verified] Constraints are Python functions mapping 3D keypoints to a cost. A hierarchical optimizer (SciPy dual annealing with SLSQP) solves in about 1 s for the first solve. Later solves use the local optimizer warm-started at about 10 Hz.
  - [verified] Automatic ReKep (VLM-generated) reaches 44.3% total success across 7 tasks (10 trials each) against VoxPoser at 10.0%. Hand-annotated constraints reach 68.6%. With disturbances, 26.7% (auto) against 6.7% (VoxPoser).
  - [verified] Failure analysis: the point tracker is the largest error source, keypoint proposal and the VLM (for example 'referring to incorrect keypoints') are considerable, and the optimizer contributes relatively little.
  - [partly] Limitations: a rigidity assumption, dependence on tracking, and fixed per-task stage skeletons. Replanning with a different structure would need keypoint proposal and the VLM to run at high frequency, which the paper calls a considerable computational challenge. No VLM latency numbers were found in the main text; appendix A.11 was not checked.
- **Pose Generation for Social Robots in Conversational Group Formations** — Marynel Vazquez, Alexander Lew, Eden Gorevoy, Joe Connolly, 2022, Frontiers in Robotics and AI, vol. 8 (published January 2022). https://doi.org/10.3389/frobt.2021.703807
  - Relevance: The classical proxemics baseline for a conversation-group stop pose (o-space fit, personal-space and free-space terms, sampled distribution via mean shift). It supports a geometric prior and a set output with no VLM. It also shows a pure geometric method fails on large groups and across walls, which are known weak spots of our solver and our weakest situation (conversation groups).
  - [verified] Fit a circle (one person: centre d in front; two: midpoint; three or more: orthogonal distance regression) or, when at least 5 points allow, an ellipse (the authors sometimes default to circles). Minimise a weighted loss of shape deviation, personal space and non-free cells with Powell's method, then face the centre.
  - [verified] A Wasserstein GAN with two GNN streams (group arrangement and proxemics from the map) is the data-driven alternative. A distribution of poses comes from many initialisations or latents, then mean shift picks the mode.
  - [partly] On the Cocktail Party test set both proposed methods beat Yang et al. 2017 on Circ. Fit, Not Free and Center Dist., not on every metric; the geometric method had the fewest personal-space violations (0.01 vs 0.28 for Yang, 0.38 for WGAN). In the 60-participant study WGAN scored 5.01 and geometric 4.81 of 7 (small, p=0.002); geometric rated higher for groups of 2-3, WGAN for 4-6.
  - [verified] Limitations: evaluation considered simulated interactions only (no real-world HRI); the geometric method's hyperparameters prioritising personal/intimate space impaired finding gaps for larger groups; in one shown case (9 people) it proposed a pose separated from the group by a wall.
- **Where Should I Join? Robot Group Joining via Language-Guided Goal Prediction** — Project mentor's group (authors not checked), 2026, arXiv 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28467
  - Relevance: The closest task match (queue, conversation and audience, pose (x, y, theta) as a distribution). It gives the numbers for prompted VLMs and for latency of a trained alternative, and states that it uses the environment only as a feasibility constraint. It does not compare with an explicit constraint solver, so our constraint-plus-solver method fills that comparison gap.
  - [verified] Task: from an RGB-D observation and a language instruction, predict a distribution over robot poses (x, y, theta). A trained People Encoder with FiLM and a pose decoder outputs an (energy, cos, sin) map and the minimum-energy location is the pose. The benchmark is 42 offline scenarios (15 conversation, 15 queue, 12 audience).
  - [verified] End-to-end pose validity: trained model 89.76 +/- 4.21% in 1.108 s. GPT-5.5 few-shot 59.76% in 49.25 s, Gemini 3 Flash few-shot 53.57% in 27.80 s, Gemini 3.1 Pro zero-shot 46.67% in 27.94 s. Grounding F1 is similar across methods (0.8125 trained against 0.8311 GPT-5.5).
  - [partly] Real robot (Boston Dynamics Spot, 5 trials per scenario, four scenarios): 18/20 successes (seated and standing conversation 5/5, static queue 4/5, dynamic queue 4/5), 0 collisions. The paper attributes the two failures to 'controller and detection errors'. The overly conservative audience-distance behaviour ('lowest energy to the outermost arc') is a separate limitation noted in the offline discussion, not a cause of the real-robot failures, since no audience scenario was run on the robot.
  - [verified] The environment is only a feasibility constraint ('restricts infeasible regions but does not explicitly prescribe target positions'). The paper names this a limitation. Per-group-type pose validity is only in Fig. 8.
  - [verified] Simulation success: proposed 100/100/90/100/66.67 over scenarios a-e (91.3% mean) against VLM-Nav 0/13.33/55/33.33/16.67 and re-implemented VLM-Social-Nav 0/50/30/60/0. Only 5-9 repeats per scenario.

## 3. Non-VLM perception: people, heading, groups

**Summary (not checked):** Non-VLM perception for one RGB-D image is now mature and cheap relative to the project's 16-60 s VLM calls. Detection is solved at the level the project needs: COCO-trained detectors (YOLO11 is AGPL-3.0; RT-DETR and D-FINE are Apache-2.0, per Roboflow's comparison pages, a secondary source) run in milliseconds. SAM 3 gives text-prompted instance masks ("person"), but it is large (848M parameters, a CUDA 12.6+ / PyTorch 2.7+ install; Ultralytics lists 3.45 GB weights and 2.9 s per image on an RTX PRO 6000, so it is probably usable but slow on the 8 GB RTX 4060). Because depth is exact, a mask or box plus depth gives a person's 3D position directly.

The weak link is facing direction, which the constraints method needs for views, groups and "visible to a person". Options: MEBOW-style body-orientation regression (MAE 8.4 deg, 96.9% within 30 deg on real COCO crops with ground-truth boxes); whole-body 2D pose with a facing rule from shoulders and hips (RTMW; the mentor's own paper does this); or mesh recovery with a global rotation (Multi-HMR 2, SAM 3D Body). Neither mesh paper reports facing-direction accuracy.

For groups, classic F-formation algorithms take position plus orientation and are fast. GCFF scores F1 0.89 (T=2/3) and 0.71 (T=1) across five datasets, and DANTE (learned) scores about 0.73 vs 0.64 at T=1 on Cocktail Party. Both degrade with noisy orientation and use parameters tuned per dataset.

The mentor's group-joining paper already uses YOLOv10, RTMW, spectral partitioning and a learned ranker (grounding in 0.94 s, 89.8% valid poses). That is the main precedent and comparison.

I found no direct measurement of real-photo detectors on game-quality avatar renders. The literature covers only the reverse direction (train synthetic, test real). That test is cheap to run on the project's own labelled scenes.

**Surprises (not checked):** The mentor's own paper already uses a conventional pipeline (YOLOv10 for people, RTMW for body orientation, spectral clustering for groups) and reports about 1 s per query, so the 'VLM-only' pipeline is the outlier on speed. The proposal's 'fraction of the time' expectation is easier to meet with these parts than with VLM calls. / On the standard group-detection benchmarks, a learned model (DANTE) beat the 2015 training-free GCFF by only 0.01-0.09 F1 at the strict threshold, and the Cocktail Party difference was not significant. Better perception inputs may matter more than a better grouper. / SAM 3 is accurate (LVIS box AP 53.6, COCO box AP 56.4) but is the slowest and heaviest option listed (Ultralytics: 2.9 s per image on an RTX PRO 6000 versus milliseconds for YOLO-class detectors), and a person-only task does not need open vocabulary. / The mesh-recovery papers report no facing-direction metric at all, although facing is what a social-goal solver needs; the older MEBOW (2020) remains the only dedicated number I found (8.4 deg MAE on real crops). / Evidence on the sim-to-real gap runs the wrong way for this project: nearly all work studies synthetic-trained models on real images, and none tested a COCO-trained detector on avatar renders.

**Open questions (not checked):** No direct measurement exists of how real-photo-trained detectors, pose estimators or orientation models perform on game-quality avatar renders (Rocketbox in Blender or Isaac Sim). The searches only found the reverse direction (train on synthetic, test on real: ViPeD/GTA V, MOTSynth, CARLA studies). A small labelled test on the project's own scenes would be a cheap, publishable measurement. / SAM 3 and Multi-HMR 2 latency and VRAM on an 8 GB RTX 4060 are not reported anywhere I found. Only data-centre numbers (H200 30 ms; V100 4-20 fps) or an RTX PRO 6000 (2.9 s per SAM 3 image) exist. / Facing-direction accuracy of mesh-recovery models (Multi-HMR 2, SAM 3D Body, CameraHMR) is not reported, and I found no benchmark of body orientation in crowds or lines (people seen from behind, partial occlusion). MEBOW has no obvious 2025-2026 successor. / How group detectors (GCFF, DANTE) behave when orientation comes from an estimator instead of annotation, and on lines or audiences rather than conversations. No F-formation detection paper newer than 2022 turned up in the searches, and the mentor's paper reaches grouping by spectral clustering plus a learned ranker instead. / How a probabilistic perception output (detection confidence, orientation uncertainty, group-membership probability) could feed the proposal's 'distribution over poses'. Nothing found on propagating perception uncertainty into a geometric goal solver. / Licences were not checked at source for: SAM License terms (SAM 3, SAM 3D Body), Multi-HMR 2 code and its Anny/SMPL-X weights, RTMW weights. YOLO11 AGPL-3.0 and RT-DETR/D-FINE Apache-2.0 come from Roboflow/Ultralytics comparison pages, not the repos. Per-class person AP and latency on an 8 GB GPU for YOLO11, RT-DETR, D-FINE, Grounding DINO, OWLv2 and YOLO-World were not found.

- **Where Should I Join? Robot Group Joining via Language-Guided Goal Prediction** — Fang, Wang, Lee, Hsu, 2026, arXiv cs.RO (Sep 2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28467
  - Relevance: The mentor's paper is the closest non-VLM pipeline: a detector plus a pose estimator for facing, spectral clustering for groups, and a learned ranker, at about 1 s per query. It is the baseline to position against, and it shows that detection and orientation can be off-the-shelf parts. Its offline benchmark does not state whether detections are ground truth or detector output (per my read of the page). Note that I read it through a summarising fetch, not the PDF.
  - [verified] On the real robot, people are detected with YOLOv10, and body orientation is estimated with RTMW, a real-time multi-person 2D/3D whole-body pose estimator.
  - [verified] Group hypotheses come from recursive spectral partitioning (local-scaling spectral clustering, Fiedler-vector thresholds swept between the 10th and 90th percentiles) and are ranked by a SigLIP2-based ranker; grounding takes 0.936 s.

- **SAM 3: Segment Anything with Concepts** — Meta AI (SAM team), 2025, arXiv (v2 HTML read). https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16719
  - [verified] A detector and a tracker share one vision encoder; the detector is DETR-based and conditioned on text, geometry and image exemplars; a presence head separates 'is the concept present' from localisation.
  - [verified] LVIS box AP 53.6 (OWLv2* 45.5, which is partially trained on LVIS; plain OWLv2 35.2; Grounding DINO-T 20.5; DINO-X 52.4), COCO box AP 56.4 (DINO-X 56.0). SA-Co/Gold cgF1 54.1 vs 72.8 human, best baseline OWLv2* 24.6. LVIS mask AP: text says 48.8, Table 1 says 48.5.
  - [verified] The paper itself quotes only 30 ms per image with 100+ detected objects on an H200. The Ultralytics figures are third-party and should be cited to that page.

Weights are gated on Hugging Face (Ultralytics page confirms).
- **Multi-HMR 2: Multi-Person Camera-Centric Human Detection, Mesh Recovery and Tracking** — Fiche, Weinzaepfel, Brégier, Baradel (NAVER LABS Europe), 2026, arXiv (12 Jun 2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14841
  - Relevance: A one-pass multi-person detector that also gives metric 3D placement and SMPL-X-style meshes with pelvis orientation (a facing source), with no intrinsics needed. Trained on synthetic data (BEDLAM, Anny-One), so a synthetic-to-synthetic fit is plausible for Rocketbox renders. Licence, VRAM and facing accuracy are unverified.

- **F-formation Detection: Individuating Free-standing Conversational Groups in Images (GCFF)** — Setti, Russell, Bassetti, Cristani, 2015, PLOS ONE. https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.2702
  - Relevance: A tiny, training-free group detector that takes (x, y, orientation) per person, so it could sit behind a detector and an orientation estimator and replace the VLM's group reading, or serve as a cross-check.   - [verified] The input is each person's position and head orientation; the method assumes both are available from tracking and head-orientation algorithms. It is built on graph cuts over transactional-segment overlap.

- **Improving Social Awareness Through DANTE: A Deep Affinity Network for Clustering Conversational Interactants** — Swofford, Peruzzi, et al., 2020, J. ACM, Vol. 37, No. 4. https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.12910
  - Relevance: The learned alternative to GCFF, with a modest gain, over little data and a small model. Whether it would transfer to simulated avatars is unknown.
  - [verified] DANTE learns pairwise affinities from position and head orientation, using a set-based social-context input so it can handle a varying number of people. The affinities are then clustered in a graph clustering framework, which yields groups of any size from dyads up.

  - [verified] Orientation input is head orientation, chosen for compatibility with established datasets (footnote 1); the authors say future work should consider both body and head orientation. In Sec. 4.5 they conclude that their approach improves the state of the art 'when the input spatial data has reasonable quality'.
- **RTMW: Real-Time Multi-Person 2D and 3D Whole-body Pose Estimation** — Jiang et al. (OpenMMLab / Shanghai AI Laboratory), 2024, arXiv. https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.08634
  - Relevance: The pose estimator the mentor's paper uses. 2D keypoints plus depth give 3D shoulder and hip points, so facing can come from a plain geometric rule (no neural orientation model), and it is cheap enough for the 8 GB GPU or even CPU. Whether it works on avatar renders is unmeasured.

  - [verified] Table 3, CPU only (model unnamed), ONNXRuntime, flip off: RTMW-l takes 35.3 ms (7.9 GFLOPs, 66.0 AP) at 256x192 and 47.6 ms (17.7 GFLOPs, 70.1 AP) at 384x288. No GPU latency, and no speed numbers for m or x.
  - [verified] It is a top-down method, so a separate person detector is needed; the paper says top-down methods suit scenes with no more than about 6 people. A 3D variant predicts z offsets relative to a root point, and orientation is not discussed.

## 4. VLM spatial placement: candidates, marks, verifiers, pointing models
**Summary (not checked):** Evidence for this angle is thin where the project needs it most. There are many pointing benchmarks and models (Where2Place, RefSpatial Placement, RoboSpatial context, Point-Bench), but they ask for a point in an empty region next to an object, with no people, no social rule and no pose. They are still useful as proxies. They show that general VLMs emitting raw image coordinates are poor at free-space pointing. GPT-4o scores about 29% on Where2Place and 5.7% on RoboSpatial-Home context. Gemini-2.5-Pro scores about 24-32% on RefSpatial Placement, depending on who ran it. Models trained on pointing data are better: RoboPoint 46.8%, RoboBrain-2.0-32B 73.6% on Where2Place, RoboRefer-2B-RFT 54% on Placement. The same models still reach only 31% on RoboSpatial context. Evidence that numbered candidate marks beat coordinates is mixed. LocateBench shows letter choice beating coordinates by 20-44 points for most models, but only for object boxes. MOKA justifies marks by assertion and reports no marks-vs-coordinates ablation. VLMnav shows annotated candidate actions help (SR 50.4% vs 29.8% text-only), and the largest single gain came from geometry-filtered candidates (33.2% without). PIVOT, which iterates over sampled marks, was the weakest baseline there. Iteration and parallel calls give small, noisy gains. I found no controlled study of sample-and-vote over points, VLM critics for placements, or BEV vs egocentric for free-space pointing. The implication for the project is that a candidate-mark variant is cheap and well motivated: the solver's top-5 places could be rendered as numbered marks and a VLM asked to choose or verify. Geometry should keep authority over exclusions. A pointing model such as Molmo or RoboBrain could serve only as the people or target locator, and would need the owner's OK for local weights. Latency favours choosing from marks, since the output is short.

**Surprises (not checked):** Even strong models are near chance on the free-space benchmarks: GPT-4o 5.7% on RoboSpatial-Home context and 29% on Where2Place, and Molmo 0.1% on RoboSpatial context despite being a pointing-trained model. / Molmo, a top performer on object pointing, is weak at placement: RefSpatial Placement 14.7% for Molmo-72B vs 24.2% for Gemini-2.5-Pro. Pointing to an object does not transfer to pointing to empty space. / The same model scores differently in different papers: Gemini-2.5-Pro on RefSpatial Placement is 24.21 in the RoboRefer paper and 31.73 in the RoboBrain 2.0 report; RoboBrain also says it screened out incorrect Where2Place cases. Cross-paper comparisons need care. / Chain-of-thought and in-context examples did not help (and for Gemini hurt) pointing in PointArena's ablation, which echoes this project's own result that pointing aids and reasoning do not help. / Numbered letters beating coordinates is not universal: Claude-3 Opus did better with coordinates in LocateBench (38.6 vs 31.3). This matters because Claude is on the project's model panel. / In VLMnav the biggest effect came from a geometric candidate proposer (about 17 SR points), larger than the effect of the visual annotation, and PIVOT-style iteration was the weakest variant.

**Open questions (not checked):** No controlled comparison found of numbered candidate marks vs raw coordinates for FREE-SPACE goals (only LocateBench for object boxes, and MOKA states the claim without an ablation). / No study found of sample-and-vote or clustering over repeated VLM points (self-consistency for pointing), including whether the spread of points is a usable confidence or a distribution over goals. RoboPoint states it has no confidence output. / No zero-shot VLM critic or verifier of a candidate stopping place was found; the closest work is VLM-informed path selection (2602.09002) with a fine-tuned verifier and only agreement numbers. Whether a VLM can verify a geometrically proposed place better than it can propose one is untested here. / No benchmark found where the correct free-space point depends on people (lines, groups, gaze); Where2Place, RefSpatial and RoboSpatial are all object-relational. / BEV or top-down render vs egocentric input for goal choice: only indirect evidence found (InfiniBench and Disjoint-3DQA, from search snippets and not read as primary sources; BEACON uses BEV output). Not tested for pointing. / Gemini Robotics-ER 1.5/1.6 and Molmo2/MolmoPoint pointing numbers were not read from primary sources (blog charts not text-readable); MolmoPoint's 70.7% PointBench claim is from the abstract only. / Did not read MOKA beyond its prompt ablations, nor SoM and CoPa in full; no numbers for CoPa or SoM were verified.

  - Relevance: Introduces Where2Place, the standard free-space pointing benchmark, and shows general VLMs (GPT-4o) are weak at it. It is a number to compare against, and a candidate specialised point predictor for lifting with depth. It gives no confidence estimate, which matters for a goal distribution.

  - [verified] Same task with four candidate boxes, two prompt formats: letter choice (ABCD) vs coordinates. GPT-4o 81.2 vs 45.4, Gemini-1.5-pro 73.3 vs 29.4, GPT-4T 59.0 vs 38.9, LLaVA-1.6 Mistral 53.7 vs 30.1 (1,317 examples; human accuracy 95%).

- **End-to-End Navigation with Vision-Language Models: Transforming Spatial Reasoning into Question-Answering (VLMnav)** — Goetting, Singh, Loquercio, 2024, PMLR 2025 (CoRL-track proceedings). https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.05755
  - Relevance: A controlled ablation of how candidate places are proposed and annotated for a zero-shot VLM navigator. In 13.9% of ObjectNav runs the VLM stopped 1-1.5 m from the target and counted as failure, suggesting weak distance estimation. All experiments use Gemini Flash. Limitations section: large drop without sliding, and specialized systems do better.
- **PIVOT: Iterative Visual Prompting Elicits Actionable Knowledge for VLMs** — Nasiriany et al., Google DeepMind, 2024, ICML 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07872
  - Relevance: The reference method for sample-mark-select-refine over points, with parallel calls as a form of self-consistency. Gains are modest and need extra calls, so with 15-60 s calls per query the latency cost is high. Its stated failure (correct reasoning attached to the wrong number) applies to any numbered-mark scheme.
  - [verified] Method: about 10 candidate samples per iteration drawn as numbered marks, the VLM picks the best, sampling distributions are isotropic Gaussians refit each round; experiments use 1-3 iterations and 3 parallel calls with outputs aggregated.
  - [verified] Offline L2 (Table 3): in-view 0.21 (1 iteration, no parallel) down to 0.17 (2 or 3 iterations, 3 parallel). Out-of-view 0.44 down to 0.37-0.39, with no clear gain from more iterations.   - [verified] More samples gave better first answers but worse optimisation because of occlusion and crowding of marks.
  - [verified] Stated limitations: no reliable use of depth, and the VLM sometimes reasons correctly but links the reasoning to the wrong arrow number.
- **RoboRefer: Towards Spatial Referring with Reasoning in Vision-Language Models for Robotics (RefSpatial-Bench)** — Zhou et al. (BAAI and collaborators), 2025, NeurIPS 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04308

  - [verified] RefSpatial-Bench success (Location / Placement / Unseen): Gemini-2.5-Pro 46.96 / 24.21 / 27.14; Molmo-72B 45.77 / 14.74 / 21.24; Molmo-7B 21.91 / 12.85 / 12.23; RoboPoint 22.87 / 9.27 / 8.40; RoboRefer-2B-RFT 52.00 / 54.00 / 41.56.
  - [verified] The benchmark has Location and Placement subsets of 100 images each plus 77 unseen-combination images; over 70% of samples need multi-step reasoning (up to 5 steps).
  - [verified] The paper says SFT overfits the training distribution and RFT generalises better to unseen combinations. In Table 2, RoboRefer-2B-SFT scores 33.77 on Unseen against 41.56 for 2B-RFT, a gap of 7.79 points. The text states 9.1% absolute, which does not match the table gap. - **RoboSpatial: Teaching Spatial Understanding to 2D and 3D Vision-Language Models for Robotics** — Song et al. (author list not verified), 2024, CVPR 2025 (venue not verified). https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16537
  - Relevance: Its 'spatial context' questions (where can an object be placed in an empty region) are the best match to free-space goal choice.   - Relevance: Compares image-space pointing by GPT-4o and pointing models against a depth-aware BEV heatmap output for a navigation goal, with a measure of how often the point lands inside static structure. ## 5. Crowded and confusing social scenes

**Summary (not checked):** I found no study that measures how a prompted VLM's goal or group inference changes as the number of people in the frame rises. The evidence is indirect, and that is a gap in itself.

What exists:
- Counting. Fine-tuned VLM counting error grows superlinearly with density (CrowdCue). Counts stay usable below about 200 people, which is far above any room-scale scene.
- Referring to one person among many. Prompted VLMs have low recall on multi-person referring and often draw a box for a person who is not there (HumanRef).
- Social scene understanding in dense scenes. In the one VQA benchmark on dense crowds (SocialNav-SUB, mean 6.65 people), the best VLM reached 0.62 agreement with humans against 0.64 for a rule-based baseline. The authors' own caveat is that rule-based and reasoning baselines may be lower bounds.
- Group inference. Prompted VLMs group people correctly in 50 to 73% of 50 scenarios (GSON, 2 to 4 people per group). On JRDB-Social, 2022-era MLLMs score poorly on holistic counting and group-level context.

Benchmarks vary density mainly by simulated pedestrian count. HabiCrowd reports 0.1 to 0.5 people per square metre, Social-HM3D has up to 6 people, and DPed-VLN has 1 to 6. They score trajectories, not stopping goals. None labels an acceptable region around a target, none has queues or groups as ground truth, and none has occlusion or same-kind distractors. Group-approach data is tiny (CongreG8: 38 robot trials, three-person groups). Procedural group generators exist (M3Act), but they target recognition rather than navigation goals.

Implications:
- The project's own crowd generator, with labelled polygons, is a real contribution: a systematic density, group-size and distractor sweep with exact goal regions appears to be unmeasured.
- The geometric solver is the right place to test degradation. The known crowd failures (last-in-line, merged bystanders, a single personal-space radius) should be tested under the project's generator first, which is cheaper than model calls.
- Perception recall, not reasoning, is the likely bottleneck as crowds grow. This fits HumanRef's low-recall finding and the project's own people-recall numbers.

**Surprises (not checked):** On SocialNav-SUB, a simple rule-based baseline (0.64) beat every prompted VLM, and the weakest category for VLMs was spatial relations rather than social reasoning. In the social category o4-mini (0.66) was close to the average human (0.62). / In CrowdCue, feeding a detector's density heatmap to the VLM made counting worse (96.26 vs 81.64 MAE), while the same information as a printed integer helped (62.65). The form in which the non-VLM signal reaches the VLM mattered more than the signal. / In HumanRef, general VLMs rarely refuse: Qwen2.5-VL-7B correctly rejects an absent referent only 7.1% of the time. A crowded scene with a missing or look-alike target may therefore produce a confident wrong grounding rather than a refusal. / HabiCrowd's densities (0.1 to 0.5 people per m2) are mild next to a queue, where people stand about 0.5 to 1 m apart (the project's solver already misbehaves below 1.41 m spacing). A real crowd stress test sits above what the main benchmarks cover. / The Falcon authors note that on Social-HM3D a low-success method had the best personal-space compliance, so social-comfort metrics alone can reward not completing the task. This supports success plus supplementary metrics, as in this project's scoring.

**Open questions (not checked):** No paper found measuring prompted-VLM people detection or group-membership recall as a function of the number of people in an indoor, robot-height RGB-D view (only counting at hundreds of people, or single benchmarks at fixed density). / No benchmark found with ground-truth acceptable standing regions or stopping poses in crowds, queues or groups. Closest is human approach trajectories (CongreG8, 38 robot trials, three-person groups), so goal-region labels in crowds appear unexplored. / No evidence found on VLM recall of the last person in a line, or on separating line members from bystanders, when many people are present. / No evidence found on VLM or detector accuracy with partial occlusion among people, or with same-kind distractors (several parcels, several people matching the description) in a one-shot setting; HumanRef has multi-instance cases but not robot-view occluded crowds. / No 2026 paper found combining VLMs with F-formation or group detection; GSON (2025, GPT-4o on marked people) remains the closest. / How Isaac Sim's own people and crowd tools control density and group structure was not verified. HuNavSim 2.0 states Isaac Sim support but I did not confirm crowd or group generation. Arena-Rosnav, SEAN, Menge, PedSim were not read in primary sources. / Details of Social-HM3D and DPed-VLN were read only from abstracts or short extracts (up to 6 humans, 1 to 6 pedestrians, ORCA-controlled). Per-density results and how counts are sampled were not found. / JRDB-Social group counts and group-detection scores for modern VLMs were not found. SocNavBench, THOR-MAGNI and CrowdNav++ were not read in full.

- **SocialNav-SUB: Benchmarking VLMs for Scene Understanding in Social Robot Navigation** — authors not recorded, 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08757
  - Relevance: The closest evidence on VLM scene understanding in dense social scenes. The probability-of-agreement figure is a yardstick for how weak prompted VLMs are in crowds and why a rule-based or geometric component can match them. It does not vary density systematically, and it scores VQA answers rather than goals.
  - [verified] The benchmark uses 60 SCAND scenes with 4,968 unique questions in spatial, spatiotemporal and social categories. Scenes average 6.65 people (SD 2.80, range 1 to 13), and answers come from human labels (153 Prolific participants, at least 5 per scenario).
  - [verified] Probability of agreement with humans (all categories) was 0.62 for o4-mini, 0.58 for Gemini 2.0, 0.54 for Gemini 2.5, 0.50 for GPT-4o and 0.46 for LLaVa-Next-Video. The rule-based baseline scored 0.64, the average human 0.60 and the human oracle 0.74.
  - [verified] Failure rate (the chosen answer had zero human probability) was 33.78% for LLaVa-Next, 23.63% for GPT-4o, 15.00% for Gemini 2.0, 9.02% for Gemini 2.5 and 6.42% for o4-mini. Qualitative failures include left/right errors and a distant person judged as one to avoid.
  - [partly] The main Limitations section names two limits: SCAND only (university campus), and a limited set of models and scenarios. The 'by no means comprehensive' rule-based remark is in the appendix (7.10, Rule-Based Baseline Details), and the 'lower bounds for higher-end model variants' remark is in a Table 11 caption (7.8), not in the Limitations discussion.
- **Referring to Any Person (HumanRef benchmark and RexSeek)** — authors not recorded, 2025, ICCV 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08507
  - Relevance: Direct evidence for the 'refer to one person among many' failure. It shows prompted VLMs have low recall on multi-person referring and hallucinate targets that are absent, which matters for target grounding and for the people-listing call in a crowd. It tests general VLMs of 2025, not the school models.
  - [verified] The HumanRef benchmark has 6,000 referring expressions over 5,732 images (1,000 per subset: attribute, position, interaction, reasoning, celebrity, rejection). Each expression refers to 2.2 people on average, and the training set has 103,028 statements.
  - [verified] Average DensityF1 over subsets is 56.2 for Qwen2.5-VL-7B (recall 52.5, precision 68.5), 31.9 for InternVL-2.5-8B (recall 29.8) and 72.6 for Molmo-7B-D. The detector-plus-LLM RexSeek-7B reaches 82.3. Taking every person box gives precision 31.0 and DensityF1 18.9.
  - [verified] On rejection (the described person is absent), Qwen2.5-VL-7B correctly returns no box 7.1% of the time, Ferret-7B 2.0% and Molmo-7B-D 68.6%. The paper says 'when a referring expression corresponds to multiple instances, recall drops significantly' and that models 'tend to predict a bounding box' regardless of whether the referent is present.
- **CrowdCue: Specialist-Cue Conditioning for Vision-Language Crowd Counting** — authors not recorded, 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23012
  - Relevance: A recent, quantitative picture of how a VLM's counting error grows with the number of people. It also shows that a detector's integer count fed to the VLM as a cue reduced error, which is a precedent for pairing a non-VLM perception module with a VLM. The densities are far above room scale and the VLM was fine-tuned, so it only supports the trend.
  - [verified] For a LoRA-fine-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-7B (single image), mean absolute error by true count was 8.99 for 0 to 50 people, 16.20 for 50 to 200, 52.89 for 200 to 500, 144.20 for 500 to 1,000 and 448.56 above 1,000. The authors call the profile 'sharply superlinear'.

  - [partly] Printing the P2PNet count as digits in a second image (CrowdCue-V) lowered overall MAE from 81.64 (SFT single-image baseline) to 62.65. The heatmap as an extra image gave 96.26, worse than the baseline. The sparse-bucket harm is documented for the text-cue variant (CrowdCue-T), not CrowdCue-V: sparse MAE was 16.78 with the correct cue against 8.99 for the baseline, which is nearly double, not slight.
  - [verified] Stated limitations include: 512x512 input only; Qwen2.5-VL-7B as the headline VLM, with a preliminary InternVL3-8B check (MAE 93.08 to 59.96 with the text cue) while LLaVA-OneVision-7B failed the format prerequisite; and only the three headline configurations replicated across three seeds, with all others single runs.
- **GSON: A Group-based Social Navigation Framework with Large Multimodal Model** — authors not recorded, 2025, IEEE RA-L. https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.18084
  - Relevance: The nearest published use of a VLM for group detection with marked people. It gives a prompted-VLM grouping accuracy to compare against, and it uses numbered visual marks plus a geometric group space (convex hull and ellipses), similar to this project's people call plus solver. Its scenes are small.
  - [verified] Each tracked pedestrian is marked on the image (bounding box, Segment Anything mask, tracking ID), and GPT-4o is prompted to infer groups from behaviours such as queuing, photography and chatting. To limit latency the LMM is queried only when a keyframe (most people in the last 3 seconds) changes.
  - [verified] On 50 scenarios with each image run 10 times per model, all four LMMs (GPT-4v, GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5-pro, Claude 3.5-sonnet) exceeded 50% correct grouping. GPT-4o was best at 73%, and nearly 90% when counting groupings with harmless extra members.
  - [verified] Group members' positions form a convex hull approximated by ellipses and treated as a high-cost region on the costmap. If the global path enters it, BIT* replans to a goal outside it.
  - [partly] v1 cites low LMM inference speed (abstract) and says LMMs are poor at accurate grounding. Real-world tests used one robot (EAI-Bot E2) and groups of 2 to 4 people. Future work lists denser interactions and environments, and distilling into smaller models. The paper does not list the small evaluation size or the single robot as limitations. The '50 scenarios' figure belongs to the LMM benchmark, not the real-world tests.

  - Relevance: Shows how a benchmark varies crowd density (people per square metre) and reports success by density bucket. The same density bucketing could organise the project's crowd sweep. It evaluates path-following policies, not stopping goals or groups.
  - [verified] It uses 40 virtual humans over 480 HM3D scenes (400/40/40 split) with density from 0.1 to 0.5 humans per m2 (mean 0.226, versus 0.143 real-world). It adds Collisions per Distance as a metric and evaluates point-goal and object-goal navigation.

  - [partly] The paper notes that Habitat 2.0 constraints allow only rigid human movements. In Table V the baselines reach about 90.7 to 94.6% point-goal SR but only about 12.3 to 20.8% object-goal SR. Whether the paper states that baselines lack explicit human dynamics was not confirmed.
- **Learning from Synthetic Human Group Activities (M3Act)** — authors not recorded, 2023. https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16772
  - Relevance: A worked example of procedurally generating many labelled multi-group scenes: group count, group size, spacing and camera are drawn from stated ranges, with group IDs and 3D annotations given for free. It is a template for the crowded-scene generator extension. It targets activity recognition and has no robot goals or reachability.
  - [verified] The number of groups is drawn as UniformRange(1, MaxNumGroups), each with one of six group activities. Characters per group are drawn from a range, spacing perturbation is Uniform(-0.25 x interval, 0.25 x interval), and rotation perturbation is Uniform(-45, 45) degrees.

- **JRDB-Social: A Multifaceted Robotic Dataset for Understanding of Context and Dynamics of Human Interactions Within Social Groups** — authors not recorded, 2024, CVPR 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.04458
  - Relevance: The main robot-view dataset with human-labelled social groups and group-level text, plus an early test of multimodal LLMs on group structure. - **A dataset of human and robot approach behaviors into small free-standing conversational groups (CongreG8)** — authors not recorded, 2021, PLOS ONE. https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0247364
  - Relevance: The only dataset found that records where people and a robot actually move to join a conversation group, which is the closest thing to goal labels in groups. **Summary (not checked):** The strongest finding for this angle is that a goal predictor for social stopping can be trained on a small synthetic set and sampled in about a second. The mentor's group-joining paper (arXiv 2609.28467) does this with a learned energy map. It is not a diffusion model, but it is an energy-based, multimodal, learned goal predictor. Its stated limitations match this project's gaps: it covers only three interaction topologies, and it picks the outermost arc in audience scenes.

Compositional diffusion and energy-based models are the closest analogue of the constraint method. Diffusion-CCSP (CoRL 2023) sums the energies of diffusion models trained per constraint type, and it generalises to unseen combinations. It needs 10k-30k solved instances per task, constraints have fixed arity, and its qualitative labels come from hand-coded rules. In effect it would learn what our solver computes exactly. SoGuDiff (2609.30560) composes social style axes with per-axis classifier-free guidance. Composition holds for some axis combinations but not all: joint guidance fails for some, and groups are only implicitly modelled.

Latency is a real advantage. Few-step policies take about 17 ms (one-step flow, manipulation) to 217-468 ms (navigation diffusion and flow), against 12-62 s for our VLM calls. Navigation diffusion policies such as NoMaD and FLUX are goal-conditioned trajectory generators. They take no language, and neither encodes etiquette, so they do not fit our task directly.

Implications for the project: (a) a learned energy or heat-map head trained on generator scenes, with the VLM supplying only the people and the request, is a credible route to a distribution over poses and to latency in the 1 s range. It needs its own held-out design and must not share rules with the generator (the circularity risk); (b) compositional energy sampling is a possible multi-candidate output for the constraint method; (c) rendering an imagined outcome for a VLM to score (Dream2Real) is slow (about 6 minutes per rearrangement), so it suits offline checking more than runtime. I found no work on diffusion for goal poses given a language request and one RGB-D image.

**Surprises (not checked):** The mentor's goal predictor, which beats prompted VLMs by 30+ points, is not a diffusion model. It is a small convolutional energy-map decoder trained on only 10,000 synthetic scenes, which suggests a heavy generative model is not what is needed. / Its latency claim of sub-second inference holds for individual stages and the real-robot setting; offline grounding (0.936 s) plus goal prediction (1.108 s) is about 2 s. The learned part alone is not reliable without a geometric layer, which supports keeping the deterministic solver. / The FLUX paper says its policy lacks explicit social reasoning, and NoMaD takes no language, so current generative navigation policies do not address etiquette. The social side is covered only by trajectory-level style guidance (SoGuDiff). / Several recent papers' text disagrees with their own tables (the One-Step Flow Policy gaps; NoMaD's number of test environments), so quoted headline gains should be re-read from the tables.

The only data point is 10,000 samples for three topologies in the group-joining paper. / Whether compositional energy sampling (Diffusion-CCSP style) can use constraints stated by a VLM at inference time, rather than constraint types fixed at training time, is unexplored. SoGuDiff and ADCS fix their constraint or style axes in advance. / I found no latency or accuracy measurement of a VLM-judged 'render the robot at a candidate goal' check on a social-navigation task. The imagination work I found is manipulation, and it is slow (Dream2Real, minutes). / I did not find a generative image model used to make social-navigation scenes with exact 3D labels. The depth-ControlNet results were for other domains and mention label-accuracy and domain-shift problems. Diffusion-based crowd and social-scene generation could not be checked. / FLUX and NoMaD timings do not name the hardware or report the full pipeline, so they cannot be compared directly with the project's RTX 4060 and a VLM call. / I could not read the full PDFs of ADCS (2505.13667), ECD (2606.21646), SoGuDiff and the Performance-Preserving diffusion steering paper (2609.24317) beyond abstracts or summaries. ## 8. Small models trained on generated, auto-labelled social scenes

**Summary (not checked):** The closest precedent for this angle is the mentor's own paper (arXiv:2609.28467). It trains a small network on only 10,000 procedurally simulated formation scenes, with person position, orientation and group-membership inputs. It reports 89.8% valid joining poses against 46-60% for prompted frontier VLMs, at about 1.1 s per query. An earlier learned social cost map (arXiv:2407.10547) shows the same recipe works for queues and conversation groups with a 0.34M-parameter conv encoder-decoder trained on 500k procedural grid samples. It also documents the failure modes: straight queues only, wrong virtual obstacles in crowds, and goals near a group read as a line. Distillation work (arXiv:2602.09002, arXiv:2606.27871, ViLAM, SOPD-SocialNav) shows that small students can be tuned on VLM-generated labels. Their evidence is thin: no teacher-vs-student numbers in the first, a ~5-point gap to the teacher on a non-social task in the second. Classical and GNN group detection (DANTE) is limited by small real datasets and an assumption of known people count. I found no work that tests compositional generalisation of a learned social-placement model to a social situation absent from training, and none on sim-to-real transfer of group-level perception. The pedestrian-detection sim-to-real literature only supports the general pattern that a gap exists. Implications: (1) the project's solver output (poses with runners-up) could be complemented or replaced by a small learned head over the VLM's lifted people, trained on the existing generator, which is cheap, fast and sidesteps the 30-60 s latency. (2) Because the generator and solver share rules, a learned model trained on solver-derived labels would inherit the circularity, so it needs held-out situations fixed in advance. (3) The generalisation claim, the project's key research question, is exactly the unexplored gap, so a leave-one-situation-out training design would be a novel contribution, as long as it is run on the development situations only and the held-out group photo and lift cases stay untouched.

**Surprises (not checked):** The mentor's trained predictor beats few-shot GPT-5.5 by about 30 points (89.8% vs 59.8%) with only 10k simulated scenes, and handcrafted rules reach only 64.3% even with oracle groups, so hand-written geometry is not a safe upper bound. / A 0.34M-parameter conv net trained on purely procedural grids reaches 96-99% on queues and groups in its own distribution (arXiv:2407.10547), yet fails on slightly bent queues or 3 m spacing. In-distribution success hides narrow generalisation. / Distilling a frontier pipeline into a 4B student needed only 500 traces in LocalNav, with a roughly 5-point success gap; the data requirement for distillation may be small, though this is not a social task. / Several 2026 papers (G2-Nav, the survey) list distillation of VLM social reasoning into compact models as future work, so published distilled social-placement models are still rare.

**Open questions (not checked):** No paper found that trains a learned social-placement or group model on some social situations and tests on a different, unseen situation (e.g. train on queues and conversation groups, test on an audience or a lift crowd). Compositional generalisation across situations looks unexplored for this task. / No work found on sim-to-real transfer of group-level or role-level social perception specifically. I found only single-pedestrian detection sim-to-real studies (ViPeD, MOTSynth, diffusion-generated data), which I did not read in full. / No teacher-vs-student numbers for distilling a VLM into a small model on social placement (as opposed to path selection); the distillation papers I fetched give no such figure or give only abstract-level claims. / Whether a learned head on top of VLM-lifted people (noisy detections, missed people) is robust to the VLM's perception errors; the mentor's paper uses grounding-stage outputs, and noise-injection training is not described. / No GNN or set-transformer paper from 2025-2026 on F-formation or queue detection turned up in my searches; this may be a gap in search coverage rather than in the literature. / How many generated scenes are needed for a learned goal or cost head to reach solver-level quality: only two data points found (10k scenes in 2609.28467, 500k grid samples in 2407.10547), with no scaling curve. / Not examined: SOPD-SocialNav (arXiv:2607.19850) details beyond its abstract, HumAIN (arXiv:2607.07357), SONG (arXiv:2607.25219), and the OLiVia-Nav work named in the survey.

## 9. Uncertainty, calibration and selection over many outputs

**Surprises (not checked):** A random verifier recovers most of the gain of a heuristic verifier in spatial test-time scaling (SAT-Real: random 58.67-67.33% vs 63.33-67.33%, baseline 41.33%), so extra diverse samples may matter more than the selector. / A claimed +31.8 point gain of a consistency-based VLM selector disappears against a format-matched majority-vote control, so plain voting is a stronger baseline than usually reported. / Spatial voting over sampled points (GUI-RC) helps point-style models less than box-style models (about +0.3 to +2.5 points on ScreenSpot-v2) and can hurt on harder sets (InternVL3-8B on ScreenSpot-Pro: -0.82). / Uncertainty-method rankings do not transfer from open-weight models to API models (mean rho about +0.08, CI includes zero), so a confidence signal chosen on the school gateway models may not hold for Claude or Gemini. / One-shot verbalised confidence is the best signal for Gemini on GUI clicks (AUROC 0.966 on ScreenSpot-v2) but weak for Claude Sonnet 4.6 on some datasets (0.478-0.695), contradicting the common advice that verbalised confidence is always the weaker signal.

- **Robots That Ask For Help: Uncertainty Alignment for Large Language Model Planners** — Ren et al. (Princeton, Google DeepMind), 2023, CoRL 2023. https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01928
  - Relevance: The reference method for calibrated ask-for-help. Its recipe (candidate set, confidence score, conformal threshold, ask if the set is not a singleton) maps onto the solver's top-5 places, but it needs a scalar confidence and a labelled calibration set, and the project has no scalar score today. Its ensemble baseline costing about 20x is a warning for sampling-heavy designs.
  - [verified] Planning is posed as multiple-choice QA: the LLM proposes four candidate steps, an extra 'an option not listed here' option is added, order is randomised, and confidence is the normalised next-token probability over the five labels.
  - [verified] Calibration uses a split-conformal threshold on 1 minus the confidence of the true label, with N=400 and delta=0.01. The quantile is the ceil((N+1)(1-eps))/N empirical quantile, and the prediction set is {y : confidence >= 1-q}. The coverage guarantee is dataset-conditional. It holds with probability 1-delta=0.99 over the draw of the calibration set, and is given as a Beta-quantile bound, not a flat 'at least 1-epsilon'. Labelling takes about 4 h for the multi-step setting and 1.5 h for single-step.
  - [verified] On the multi-step tabletop hardware task at target 0.75, KnowNo reached 0.74 task success with 0.58 step-wise help, against 0.72 success and 0.72 help for Simple Set. That is 14% fewer step-wise helps and 8% fewer trial-wise helps. The mobile-manipulation hardware task used target 0.85 and showed a 14% reduction. Across all experiments, mostly simulation, the paper reports 10-24% less help. The Ensemble Set baseline needs about 20x inference time and was only run in simulation.
  - [verified] The guarantee assumes the environment is fully grounded in the text given to the LLM and that proposed actions can be executed, and that the human helps faithfully.
  - [verified] Rankings of uncertainty methods transfer within open-weight models (mean Spearman rho 0.705 over 120 pairs) but not to closed-source vendors (mean rho about +0.08, bootstrap CI [-0.219, +0.373] including zero).
  - [verified] For the API models, self-consistency (SelfCons) AUROC ranges about 0.51-0.78, and one-shot verbalised confidence ranges about 0.48-0.97 with the best case on Gemini (0.966 on ScreenSpot-v2), so neither dominates and the winner depends on the vendor.
  - [partly] The abstract reports that Disk-Normalized conformal disks shrink radii by 40-60% when UQ is calibrated, but the body examples show about 20-35%. Coverage drops under calibration-test or interface mismatch. The study uses n_samples=5, below what SafeGround-style dispersion estimates need, and covers single-step clicks only.
- **Test-Time Reinforcement Learning for GUI Grounding via Region Consistency (GUI-RC)** — Du, Yan, et al., 2025, AAAI. https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.05615
  - Relevance: A worked example of spatial self-consistency over sampled point outputs: pixel-wise vote grid, take the densest region. It transfers directly to voting over lifted 3D goal points or floor-grid cells across trials, but its own limitation (small gains for point-style models, no help when the samples are consistently wrong) matches the project's finding that marked points are mostly not near misses.
  - [verified] Training-free spatial voting: 64 predictions sampled at temperature 0.5 (top_p 0.95); each point is expanded to a 50 px square; each prediction votes on every pixel in its region; the largest contiguous region of maximum votes is the consensus.
  - [verified] Gains on ScreenSpot-v2 are modest: Qwen2.5-VL-3B 80.11 to 82.63, Qwen2.5-VL-7B 86.48 to 88.52, InternVL3-8B 80.97 to 81.68. On ScreenSpot-Pro InternVL3-8B drops 13.28 to 12.46.
  - [verified] The paper reports relatively limited gains for point-output models. It says the method struggles with confusion hallucinations, where the predicted region matches no valid UI element. Accuracy rises then plateaus with sample count, and rises then falls with temperature because regions become too dispersed.
- **It's the Decoding Format, Not the Perturbation: Auditing Consistency-Based Selection for Vision-Language Test-Time Scaling** — not extracted from the page, 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01207
  - [verified] Perturbation Grounded Selection appears to beat plain majority voting by up to +31.8 points on TextVQA with Qwen, but only against a CoT-only majority-vote baseline.
  - [verified] With a format-matched control (the same short, no-CoT draws spent on the original image), the control 'tracks or exceeds Pgs within noise on every benchmark, including the vision-required ViLP'.
  - [verified] The authors conclude that perturbation consistency is 'at best a partial diagnostic of visual dependence and, on its own, not a usable selection signal once format is controlled', and that gains against CoT-only voting 'overstate such methods'.
- **Probing the effectiveness of World Models for Spatial Reasoning through Test-time Scaling** — not extracted from the page, 2025, World Modeling Workshop 2026 (extended abstract). https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.05809
  - Relevance: Shows that in spatial test-time scaling much of the gain can come from extra diverse samples rather than from verifier quality. A candidate-and-verify layer over the solver's top 5 should therefore be tested against a random pick of the same candidates. This is a workshop abstract on a different task (view selection), so it is suggestive evidence only.
  - [verified] On SAT-Real (150 questions, InternVL3-14B baseline 41.33%), a random-scoring verifier reaches 58.67-67.33% accuracy across top-k and gamma settings, against 63.33-67.33% for the MindJourney heuristic verifier at gamma=1.
  - [verified] The proposed assertion-based verifier (ViSA) reaches 65.33-72.67% at gamma=1 on SAT-Real, but on MMSI-Bench (baseline 27.16%) 'none of the verifiers, including ours, achieve consistent scaling'. ViSA also underperforms the baseline in some configurations.
  - [verified] Random selection 'surprisingly produces lower entropy, and hence better calibration than MJ's helpfulness scoring'. The entropy analysis covers only 50 SAT-Real questions.
  - [verified] Conformal prediction on score-token log-probabilities, with R2CCP as the default among the CP methods compared (text says eight, Table 2 lists nine), alpha=0.10, 10 random seeds with 50/50 calibration/test splits.
  - [verified] All three judges reach about 32-34% exact agreement. On MLLM-as-a-Judge 68.8% of boundary-adjusted intervals span more than 75% of the scale. For LLaVA-Critic, coverage for GT=1 is 88.9% with boundary adjustment (53.0% raw), which the paper attributes to systematic judge overscoring of poor answers.
- **CUSP: Decomposable Collective Uncertainty for Multi-Agent Multimodal Reasoning** — not extracted from the page, 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05708
  - Relevance: The one result found on combining heterogeneous VLMs with uncertainty. The project already has three gateway models plus Claude and Gemini, so cross-model disagreement on the goal cell or on the person list could serve as a black-box abstain-or-ask trigger with no logits. It is a multimodal QA paper, not a spatial or robotics one, so transfer to goal poses is unproven.
  - [verified] Each model's response is mapped into a shared semantic space and pooled. The entropy of the pooled opinion splits exactly into the mean of each model's own semantic entropy plus the Jensen-Shannon divergence among models (dispersion plus disagreement). It needs no logits or calibration labels.
  - [verified] In the small-model regime of static multi-VLM ensembles, collective uncertainty reaches 0.764 AUROC for error detection and 0.889 AUARC for abstention, beating majority voting and naive selection by 4.7 to 15.8 points (margin widens with ensemble size). In the commercial-model regime, JSD is the strongest signal at 0.819 AUROC and 0.910 AUARC.
  - [partly] The pooled prediction improves accuracy over the average single model by 5.6 to 13.0 points. (Drop the claim about careless aggregation amplifying errors unless found in the full text.)
- **When to Act, Ask, or Learn: Uncertainty-Aware Policy Steering** — not extracted from the page, 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.22474
  - Relevance: A recent act/ask/intervene design that uses conformal calibration over a VLM verifier. It confirms naive VLM probabilities are overconfident and that eliciting hidden user intents then marginalising gives better calibration. This is an idea for the request-ambiguity side (which parcel, which person), though its scale (80 calibration pairs, 40 test) and its manipulation setting limit comparison.
  - [verified] A VLM verifier picks among K sampled actions plus 'none of the above'. Conformal prediction on the VLM probabilities (sequence-level calibration) gives a prediction set, and the set size decides between executing, asking a clarifying question, or requesting intervention.
  - [partly] The paper says 'Naively querying a VLM for likelihoods over multiple choices results in significantly overconfident distributions, especially in ambiguous or incapable scenarios.' Bayesian Intent with CP is reported to reach >=85% coverage while minimising clarification rate. Vanilla is poorly calibrated (overconfident or under-covering), and CoT over-asks (near 100% clarification) or loses coverage.
  - [verified] Calibration used 80 observation-instruction pairs and the test set has 40 samples (overall counts, as far as the text shows). The method assumes world-model imaginations and narrations are correct and complete. After residual training, coverage was slightly lower in simulation and on Insert Block, attributed to the difficulty of simulating contact-rich manipulation with the current world model.

## 10. Target grounding under clutter, occlusion and distractors

**Summary (not checked):** The strongest evidence says the usual fix for cluttered grounding is a split. A class-agnostic segmenter or detector proposes masks, the VLM only picks among numbered candidates, and depth is read from the whole mask rather than from the VLM's point. Set-of-Mark (SoM) showed that mask marks lift GPT-4V on RefCOCOg REC from 25.7 to 86.4, but open-source models "can hardly interpret the marks". The authors' own ablation shows proposal quality is the ceiling (75.6 vs 90.1 mIoU with ground-truth masks). SAM 3 (about 850M parameters, 30 ms per image on an H200) segments all instances of a noun phrase. It is limited to simple noun phrases, so the paper pairs it with an MLLM agent that proposes phrases and checks the masks. Gemini 2.5 Pro as the backbone gives ReasonSeg 77.0 val against the previous best 65.0. Same-kind distractors are the open problem for VLM-only grounding. On Ref-Adv, which pairs each target with at least two same-category distractors (at least three candidates per image), 72B-class models fall from about 93% on RefCOCO to 58% (Qwen2.5-VL-72B). Accuracy drops further as the distractor count rises, and chain of thought helps on average but not for every model. For people, HumanRef shows that general VLMs handle attribute and position descriptions poorly (Qwen2.5-VL-7B scores 56.2 DF1 against 82.3 for a detector-plus-MLLM). They also almost never answer "no such person" (rejection 7.1%). For lifting, taking the median depth over a mask beats a single pixel, but I found no study of depth bleed at object boundaries or behind occluders, and no 3D scene graph paper that works from one view. Implication for the project: add a cheap, optional proposal layer (SAM 3 masks, or OWLv2 or Grounding DINO boxes, then numbered marks, mask-median lifting and a visibility check). Test it first offline on recorded plan outputs. Its gains have to be shown on occluded and same-kind-distractor scenes, which the generator does not produce today.

## 11. Reinforcement fine-tuning with verifiable spatial rewards (GRPO-style) on auto-labelled scenes

**Surprises (not checked):** Lu et al. (2026) find that SFT on difficulty-filtered data matches or beats GRPO out of domain for the 7B model (62.10 vs 59.48 average OOD), and is 3.2-4.9x faster. This weakens 'RL generalises, SFT memorises' as a reason to pick RL. / In GUI-G2, adding explicit reasoning (thinking mode) lowered accuracy (88.7 vs 93.3), and the VLM-R1/Visual-RFT line still stresses reasoning. For pure pointing, rewards on the point matter more than thinking tokens; Embodied-R1's ablation, though, shows reasoning helps region-referring (65.50 vs 63.00). / Chu et al. report that RL could not repair an overfit SFT checkpoint and that RL from the base model failed without an SFT warm-up on their 11B backbone, so a two-stage recipe (a little SFT, then RL) is needed, not RL alone. / Binary point-in-region rewards lose near-miss information (GUI-G2: 87.4 binary vs 93.3 Gaussian), which matters here because the project's marked points are mostly not near misses; a dense reward would mostly help models whose answers are already close. / Reward hacking was reported in every reward-heavy paper read (VLM-R1 detection predicting all categories, Embodied-R1 two-point traces, floor-plan rooms shrunk to tiny sizes, SIRL 'persists'), including for geometric rewards.

- **Visual-RFT: Visual Reinforcement Fine-Tuning** — Ziyu Liu, Zeyi Sun et al., 2025, ICCV 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01785
  - Relevance: Closest evidence for RL vs SFT on the same tiny data with verifiable IoU rewards (about 100 samples for one-shot classification), so it addresses the hundreds-of-prompts question. A pitfall: it shows a 2B model, and some SFT baselines fall below the untuned base model.
  - [verified] Verifiable reward for detection is the sum of an IoU term, a confidence term and a format term; classification uses 0/1 accuracy plus format.
  - [verified] With Qwen2-VL-2B on COCO 8-category few-shot detection, Visual-RFT gets 33.6 / 41.5 / 40.6 / 47.4 / 46.8 mAP for 1/2/4/8/16 shots against SFT 19.5 / 21.0 / 25.2 / 30.2 / 31.3 (baseline 19.6).
  - [verified] On the out-of-domain MG dataset (2B, 4-shot), Visual-RFT reaches 61.8 mAP versus SFT 26.8 (baseline 20.6). On LISA reasoning grounding (2B) it gets 37.6 mIoU versus SFT 28.3 (baseline 26.9); 7B gives 43.9 vs 39.1 (baseline 40.4).
  - [verified] Training scale is small: the LISA reasoning-grounding setting uses 239 training images; the abstract's one-shot classification setting uses about 100 samples.
- **SFT Memorizes, RL Generalizes: A Comparative Study of Foundation Model Post-training** — Tianzhe Chu et al., 2025, ICML 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17161
  - Relevance: The standard citation for the claim that RL carries over to unseen rules and visuals while SFT does not, in a navigation setting (V-IRL). It also warns that RL needs an SFT warm-up and cannot rescue an overfit SFT checkpoint.
  - [verified] On V-IRL-VL with a visual shift (train on New York routes, test on other cities), RL raised per-step accuracy from 16.7% to 77.8% (+61.1), while SFT fell to 11.1% (-5.6). On a rule shift in V-IRL-VL, RL went from 35.7% to 45.0% while SFT fell to 2.5%.
  - [verified] SFT warm-up is needed: without it, end-to-end RL on the base model failed to improve on GeneralPoints-L, because the base model gave long unstructured outputs from which rewards could not be extracted.
  - [verified] RL could not recover OOD performance from an overly tuned (overfit) SFT checkpoint; the model collapsed to the training rule.
- **Why Does RL Generalize Better Than SFT? A Data-Centric Perspective on VLM Post-Training** — Aojun Lu, Tao Feng, Hangjie Yuan, Wei Li, Yanan Sun, 2026, arXiv preprint (a third-party listing says CVPR 2026; not verified on the arXiv page). https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10815
  - Relevance: Cuts against the idea that RL is inherently better for generalising to new situations. It says the benefit comes from implicit selection of medium-difficulty samples, which cheap difficulty-filtered SFT can replicate. Use it as the control to run before claiming an RL benefit.
  - [verified] Authors attribute RL's OOD advantage to an implicit data-filtering effect that favours medium-difficulty samples; in SFT, training on hard samples significantly degrades OOD performance.
  - [verified] Average OOD accuracy, Qwen2.5-VL-3B: SFT 56.50, GRPO 58.31, SFT on medium-difficulty data 59.02, SFT with hard data removed 59.44. For 7B: 57.62, 59.48, 60.41, 62.10. At 7B the hard-removed SFT beats GRPO by 2.62 points.
  - [partly] On Qwen2.5-VL-7B, DC-SFT (including curation time) is reported 4.9x more efficient than GRPO on ImageNet and 3.2x on RefCOCO. GRPO shows sharp drops at 300-400 steps (ImageNet-R) and 100-200 steps (Ref-L4). Hardware and timing conditions are not specified.
- **Embodied-R1: Reinforced Embodied Reasoning for General Robotic Manipulation** — Yifu Yuan et al., 2025, ICLR 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.13998
  - [verified] Reward = binary format + point-in-mask (1 if inside the ground-truth mask) + point-distance reward (linear falloff on Euclidean distance to the mask center, clipped to [0,1]; thresholds not stated in the main text). Table 9 weights: OAG 0.1/0.8/0.1, RRG 0.1/0.6/0.3. The main text says RRG is 0.1 format, 0.7 mask, 0.2 distance, which conflicts with Table 9.
  - [verified] Ablation on region-referring benchmarks (Where2Place / VABench-P): RL with reasoning 65.50 / 65.39, RL without reasoning 63.00 / 60.50, no RL (same data, SFT) with reasoning 41.25 / 47.67, no RL without reasoning 36.85 / 50.46.
  - [verified] Reward hacking: without a fixed number of points, the trace model output only two points forming a straight line; fixed by requiring exactly 8 points.
  - [verified] Compute: Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct, GRPO with 8 samples per prompt, built on EasyR1, on eight A100 40G GPUs, about 48 hours per phase; stage 2 uses about 200k pointing samples, stage 1 about 84k spatial plus 18k general samples.
- **GUI-G^2: Gaussian Reward Modeling for GUI Grounding** — Fei Tang et al., 2025, AAAI 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.15846
  - Relevance: Direct evidence on reward shaping for region targets: replacing the binary point-in-box hit with a distance-decay plus overlap reward lifted accuracy by several points. A polygon-aware analogue (distance to polygon, margin inside it) is the natural reward for this project's acceptable bands.
  - [verified] Point reward is a 2D Gaussian centred on the target with variance proportional to box size (alpha = 0.5); coverage reward uses the Bhattacharyya coefficient between predicted and target Gaussians; total reward is the sum with equal weights (nu = gamma = 1.0).
  - [verified] ScreenSpot-v2 average accuracy: sparse point reward 87.4, sparse IoU 85.8, sparse point+IoU 86.5, dense Gaussian 93.3 (+5.9 over the best sparse). A fixed-variance variant got 87.8.
  - [verified] There is no dedicated limitations section. Appendix A.3 (error analysis) and A.4 (future work) give the closest equivalent: icon errors are 76.9% of failures, and compute overhead may limit high-resolution use. Thinking mode scored lower (88.7 vs 93.3) in an ablation, though the paper reports the gap inconsistently (+5.3% in the text, +4.6 points in Table 6). Binary rewards are argued to create cliffs at box edges where gradients vanish. Random rewards lowered accuracy (Appendix A.1).
- **Solver-Informed RL: Grounding Large Language Models for Authentic Optimization Modeling (SIRL)** — Chen, Xia, Shao, Ge, Ye, 2025, NeurIPS 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11792
  - Relevance: The closest precedent for solver-in-the-loop RL: the model writes a formal problem, an external solver runs it, and the solver's feedback (valid, executes, correct) is the reward. This maps to training a VLM to write constraints for this project's fixed solver, although SIRL is text-only.
  - [verified] Reward is rule-based and verifiable from the solver: stage 1 sums format, execution and accuracy terms; stage 2 adds a bonus from the .lp file granted only when the solution is correct and uses advanced techniques.
  - [verified] Removing KL entirely lowered the OptMATH execution rate (80.1% vs 92.2% with Partial KL and 93.4% with Full KL). Partial KL gave the best accuracy (30.5%), not the best execution rate.
  - [verified] SIRL-Qwen2.5-7B reaches 60.6% macro-average pass@1 and the 32B model 68.3%, versus 65.0% for DeepSeek-V3.1 and 57.1% for OpenAI-o3 on the listed sets.
  - [verified] The authors say reward hacking persists and that results on IndustryOR and OptMATH remain limited.
- **TRL GRPOTrainer documentation (vision-language models section)** — Hugging Face TRL, 2026. https://huggingface.co/docs/trl/main/en/grpo_trainer
  - Relevance: Shows that an open toolkit trains multimodal GRPO with a custom Python reward function (where a solver call or polygon check can go) and with LoRA or 4-bit QLoRA. It gives no memory number, so feasibility on 8 GB remains untested.
  - [verified] VLMs tested with GRPOTrainer include Gemma3, LLaVA-NeXT, Qwen2-VL, Qwen2.5-VL and SmolVLM2; the quick start trains Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct with --use_peft and LoRA on q_proj and v_proj.
  - [verified] Reward functions are arbitrary callables receiving prompts, completions and any dataset column (such as ground truth), returning one float per completion; multiple rewards are summed or weighted.
  - [verified] The page has no memory-saving table. The levers are scattered across the VLM tips and the GRPOConfig reference. quantization_config is for QLoRA with peft_config, and the page's tip says to enable 4-bit quantization. beta defaults to 0.0, so the reference model is not loaded. num_generations defaults to 8. max_completion_length defaults to 512, and the quick start sets 1024. vllm_mode defaults to colocate. vllm_gpu_memory_utilization defaults to 0.3 and applies only in colocate mode. The page says VLMs are memory-intensive and advises starting with smaller batch sizes.
- **Generative Floor Plan Design with LLMs via Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards** — see arXiv listing (author list not read), 2026, arXiv preprint. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14117
  - Relevance: A worked example of a geometric verifiable reward with a hard validity gate and a documented reward-hacking failure. It supports a gate-plus-continuous-terms design and the warning that a single objective gets gamed.
  - [verified] Reward is a hard feasibility gate (invalid JSON or overlapping room polygons give zero) times the equal-weighted average of a connectivity term and a total-area term.
  - [partly] Rewarded for connectivity alone, the model collapsed geometry into tiny rooms so the reconstructed adjacency matched the prompt. This gave near-zero Compatibility but violated requested areas and hurt Realism. The area term was added to address it.
  - [verified] SFT then GRPO (TRL, 4 generations per prompt, LR 1e-6, KL 0.04) on a Llama-3.3-70B LoRA reduced overlap by 65% and compatibility error by 56% relative to SFT, averaged across tasks; the best checkpoint was usually at step 100 (about two hours on 7 nodes of 4 H100). Single seed, one backbone.

## 12. Choosing worked examples and optimising one shared prompt set across several VLMs

**Surprises (not checked):** Image content in demonstrations often matters little: perturbing demonstration images barely changed accuracy on most tasks in 2407.00902, while text perturbations hurt. If this holds for goal prediction, the 4-image cap is a smaller constraint than assumed, and text-only examples may be nearly as good. / Multimodal ICL matches text-only ICL zero-shot but degrades with few-shot demonstrations (2604.13403), and open-weights VLMs (Llama3.2-Vision, InternLM-XComposer-2.5) showed no benefit from demonstrations at all in the many-shot study (2405.09798). The project's E-over-R gain for Qwen is therefore better than the literature would predict, and a gain for GLM and DeepSeek cannot be assumed. / Heuristic example selection (nearest, diverse) did not reliably beat random in 2406.15708, and in VL-ICL Bench no example ordering won consistently. Optimising the exemplar set directly beat both heuristics. / More shots can hurt: VL-ICL Bench reports performance often rising and then falling with more shots, so using the full 3-example allowance is not automatically best. / Reflective optimisers can lose to a fixed, well-designed prompt in low-data regimes (2607.11944, a single-author preprint), and candidate count raised accuracy but multiplied variance 3.7x. Low-variance protocol matters more than the optimiser brand. / My first search snippet implied a 34.0% GEPA score was a seed-prompt comparison; the fetched abstract shows 34.0% is GEPA's GSM8K-Hard score in a MAGE comparison. I did not verify the '62.4% seed' figure and left it out.

**Open questions (not checked):** No controlled study found of worked examples for pointing, goal-placement or numeric-pose outputs in a VLM; the only localisation evidence is mean IoU in the many-shot paper, where open models did not benefit at all. The size and cause of the E-over-R gain is open here (format teaching, spatial calibration, or social knowledge). / No paper found that optimises one prompt against a panel of target models (min or mean over models); the closest are single-model optimisers plus transfer tests. Whether a shared prompt is Pareto-optimal across Qwen, GLM and DeepSeek is unexplored. / Text-only (imageless) examples versus image examples: the text-dominance findings suggest text may carry most of the signal, but no direct ablation on a spatial or goal task was found. A cheap internal test is a 3-example text-only arm versus a 3-image arm. / Examples for structured outputs: only vendor guidance, a blog summary of NuExtract (output-only examples worked) and a shared-task paper that does not isolate example effects. No ablation of example count on schema outputs or on recall of people was found. / Whether examples showing a different situation transfer to a new one: found only cross-task low-level vision work (T2T-VICL, 2511.16107, not read in full). No result on social or spatial situations. / Retrieval of similar examples (RICES-style) is incompatible with the rule of one shared fixed prompt unless the retrieval key avoids situation words; its gain (about 3 points in VL-ICL Bench) is small next to the 16-point E-over-R gain. / How multimodal prompt optimisers (MPO 2510.09201, ICLR 2026; UniAPO 2508.17890) behave on small dev sets: only abstracts read, no numbers verified. / Prompt-optimisation overfitting at 14 layouts per set: MAGE reports fixed prompts beating optimisers at 30 training items, but on a single math task with gpt-4o-mini and Llama 3.1 8B; nothing reported for vision tasks.

- **From Introspection to Best Practices: Principled Analysis of Demonstrations in Multimodal In-Context Learning** — not extracted from the fetched pages, 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.00902
  - Relevance: Closest controlled study of which parts of a multimodal demonstration matter and how to select them. Suggests demonstration text (the reasoning and answer format) carries most of the signal, and that similarity-based selection is a cheap, consistent gain; but tasks are classification, VQA and counting, not goal placement, and instruction-tuned models were not tested.
  - [verified] Perturbing demonstration images (remove, zero, randomise, permute) does not significantly lower ICL accuracy on easy and moderate tasks; the exception is key information extraction, where it can fall below zero-shot.
  - [verified] Text-driven selection (CLIP text encoder, BERTScore) improves ICL consistently across models and tasks versus zero-shot and random selection. On KIE, where visual information matters, visual-based (RICES) and dual-modality (MMICES, ALBEF) selection give substantial gains; ALBEF beat MMICES. Demonstrations are ordered by increasing similarity, most similar last.
  - [partly] Models: pretrained (not instruction-tuned) OpenFlamingo-4B/9B, IDEFICS1-9B/80B, IDEFICS2-8B, Emu1-14B, plus GPT-4o as a proprietary reference. Main analysis uses 4 and 8 shots. SEED-Bench-2 used 1 and 2 shots, and Sec. 6 uses 8-shot. In the flipped-label test, smaller models follow flipped labels, with accuracy falling well below 50%, when demonstrations are selected by text similarity. Random or visual selection did not produce this. GPT-4o keeps the factual semantic priors. The lack of instruction-tuned models is a stated limitation.
- **VL-ICL Bench: The Devil in the Details of Multimodal In-Context Learning** — Zong, Bohdal, Hospedales (University of Edinburgh), 2024, ICLR 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13164
  - Relevance: Standard benchmark for multimodal ICL across 16 image-to-text models. Gives numbers on shot scaling, example selection and order, and shows that zero-shot strength does not predict ICL ability, so one example set may help models unevenly. Its text-versus-image comparison supports considering text-only examples.
  - [partly] In Appendix B.10, CLIP-similarity retrieval is said to consistently improve on random selection, by roughly 3% in peak accuracy and ICL efficiency. It was tested on only CLEVR, TextOCR and CoBSAT, with a few models. Table 10 has exceptions: random beats all three orderings on peak accuracy for Mantis-Idefics2 on CLEVR (40.2 vs 36.0-36.5), and random roughly ties on TextOCR for InternLM-X2.5. The three orderings were tested. I did not confirm that 'no consistent winner' is the paper's wording.
  - [verified] The paper says performance often decreases again at larger shot counts (examples: GPT-4V on CLEVR Count Induction, InternLM-XComposer2 on Operator Induction). LLaVA-OneVision 0.5B and 7B perform worse than chance as shots increase on Interleaved Operator Induction and Fast Matching MiniImageNet. The 72B model 'demonstrates emergent ICL abilities'. Zero-shot performance 'is not strongly indicative of ICL ability'. In Table 2, GPT-4V Operator Induction goes from 24.0% zero-shot to 92.0% peak, and LLaVA-OneVision-72B Fast Open-Ended MiniImageNet from 0.0% zero-shot to 98.7% peak.
  - [verified] Text versions of the count and operator tasks improve more steadily with shots than image versions, which the authors attribute to lower perception difficulty and fewer tokens; chain-of-thought gave no consistent improvement.
- **Teach Better or Show Smarter? On Instructions and Exemplars in Automatic Prompt Optimization** — Xingchen Wan, Ruoxi Sun, Hootan Nakhost, Sercan O. Arik (Google Cloud AI Research), 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.15708
  - Relevance: Most directly applicable to choosing worked examples: shows that optimising exemplars can beat optimising instruction wording, that heuristics like nearest or diverse exemplars do not reliably beat random, and that optimised exemplars show a smaller validation-to-test gap. Text-only tasks (BBH, MMLU) and Google/PaLM models, so the transfer to image goals is untested.
  - [verified] Best exemplar optimisation alone gains more than 11% over the seed prompt, versus about 8% for the best instruction optimisation; the paper says it should 'almost always' run exemplar optimisation when doing prompt optimisation.
  - [verified] Heuristic exemplar selection (Nearest, Diversity) does not consistently beat Random, whereas optimisation-based selection (random search, mutation) does. On PaLM 2 BBH, No-IO + Mutation scores 72.92 vs 60.30 for No-IO + No-EO.
- **GEPA: Reflective Prompt Evolution Can Outperform Reinforcement Learning** — not extracted from the fetched pages, 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.19457
  - Relevance: Leading reflective prompt optimiser (in DSPy). Relevant mechanisms are the per-instance Pareto candidate pool (keeps prompts that win on at least one case, a fit for a mixed panel of models or layouts) and the one reported cross-model transfer result. All six tasks are text-only, so multimodal use and the cost on a 14-layout dev set are unproven.
  - [partly] Abstract: across six tasks GEPA beats GRPO by 6% on average and by up to 20% (Observation 1 says up to 19%), with up to 35x fewer rollouts. It beats MIPROv2 by over 10% (e.g. +12% on AIME-2025). On Qwen3 8B, Table 1 aggregate gains are +9.62 for GEPA, +2.61 for MIPROv2 and +3.68 for GRPO. The +12.19 and +5.64 figures are GPT-4.1 Mini results from Table 2, not Qwen3 8B.
  - [verified] Prompts optimised with Qwen3-8B transferred to GPT-4.1-mini unchanged with +9.00% aggregate gain (up to +27.67% on HotpotQA). Tasks are six text benchmarks; no multimodal task is reported.
  - [verified] On GSM8K-Hard with gpt-4o-mini (5 seeds), MAGE scores 46.4% against GEPA 34.0%, with similar variance (7.3% vs 7.0%); raising candidates from 3 to 5 lifted mean accuracy by 21.6% but multiplied variance by 3.7.
- **Many-Shot In-Context Learning in Multimodal Foundation Models** — Jiang, Irvin and colleagues (Stanford ML group); ICML 2024 workshop version listed, 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09798
  - Relevance: Shows the example benefit is model-dependent: Gemini 1.5 Pro scaled steadily, GPT-4o less stably, and two open-weights VLMs did not benefit at all. A warning that a shared example set may help the stronger model while doing nothing for the weaker ones. Also gives the only localisation numbers I found, and a way to cut call cost by batching.
  - [verified] Gemini 1.5 Pro improves with demonstrating examples on all datasets except DrugOOD Assay, log-linearly on 9 of the 10 classification datasets. On 8 of 14 datasets it kept improving up to the largest demo count tried. GPT-4o shows V-shaped curves on many datasets. Llama 3.2-Vision and InternLM-XComposer2.5 'do not benefit from the demonstrating examples'.
  - [verified] Two of the 14 datasets are object localisation (DIOR, DeepLesion), scored with mean IoU; the authors note 'most LMMs are not yet capable of accurately producing localizations'.
  - [verified] Batching up to 50 queries in one call caused minimal degradation and sometimes improved results; with many-shot prompts it cut latency about 35x and cost about 10x on HAM10000. Ablations used only Gemini 1.5 Pro.
- **Why Multimodal In-Context Learning Lags Behind? Unveiling the Inner Mechanisms and Bottlenecks** — Yu Wang, Sharon Li, 2026, ACL 2026 (Main), as listed on the arXiv page. https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.13403
  - [verified] Using identical task formulations across modalities, multimodal ICL matches text-only ICL zero-shot but degrades significantly with few-shot demonstrations.
  - [verified] Two bottlenecks: models lack reasoning-level alignment between visual and textual representations, and fail to reliably transfer learned task mappings to queries; the paper proposes a simple inference-stage method that reinforces task mapping transfer.
- **Two-Stage Prompt Optimization for Few-Shot Relation Extraction (GradPO)** — not extracted, 2026. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29639
  - Relevance: A small but concrete data point that optimised prompt edits are model-specific and transfer poorly between similar small models, which supports putting all three gateway models in the optimisation objective instead of tuning on one. Narrow setting (two 4B models, one text task, gradient-based edits), so do not over-generalise.

## 13. Agentic VLMs with tools: zoom and crop, detector calls and code over 3D geometry

**Surprises (not checked):** VADAR, the program-writing agent with a dynamic API, did not beat a direct GPT-4o answer on metric questions (40.4 vs 42.9). ## 14. Human judgments of where to stand, and metrics for scoring a goal distribution

**Summary (not checked):** The literature is thin exactly where this project needs it. I found no study in which many people choose where a robot should stop in a line, near people watching something, or at a conversation group, and report the spread of their choices.

What exists:
- Approach-distance surveys. These are mostly single-person or group-approach rating studies, such as an online survey of 181 people in three countries (Joosse-era Twente study). It reports differences between countries but no per-situation stop-point map.
- Human-labelled scene datasets. Mind Your Manners / MANNERS-DB has 15 raters per scene. SocNav1 has inter-rater kappa of 0.61 to 0.71. SocialNav-SUB used 153 online participants, with at least 5 answers per scenario.
- Metric literature from other fields: trajectory forecasting and saliency.

The closest precedent is the mentor's own group-joining preprint (arXiv 2609.28467).
- Its validity rule is the same 0.5 to 1.2 m ring or band as ours, "derived from social conventions and proxemics theory", with no human check of the rule.
- Its only human evidence is a pilot where 15 people clicked "the most suitable position". Each image was split into 500 bins and a KL divergence was reported, with few details.
- It scores a distribution by sampling 10 goal poses per scenario and counting valid ones.
- So the project's circularity problem is shared by the closest published work. A small click study on our own rendered scenes would be new and would also give the human heatmap.

On metrics, the transferable findings are these:
- Best-of-k (minADE/minFDE) is not a strictly proper scoring rule, and its value depends on k. The energy score is proper and comparable across k.
- Saliency work says the choice of KL, SIM, NSS or CC depends on whether outputs are probabilities.
- Report top-1 success, mass inside the region, and a hit-at-k curve together.
- Francis et al. (2025) ask for distributions rather than single means, and a battery of metrics.

Conformal and set-valued coverage-versus-size reporting I did not find in a usable form.

**Surprises (not checked):** The closest published work (the mentor's preprint) already uses the same 0.5-1.2 m ring and has only a 15-person click pilot as human support, so the circularity is an accepted community practice, not a project-specific weakness. A larger click study on our rendered scenes would be stronger than the published precedent. / Their human metric (KL on 500 bins from 15 clicks) is the kind Bylinskii et al. warn about: KL, SIM and IG punish zero-valued predictions where humans clicked and need regularization. With so few clicks a smoothed heatmap and NSS or CC may be more stable. This is inference, not tested. / Best-of-k is flattering twice over: it falls as k grows for the same predictions and is not strictly proper. The solver's 'any of 5 places' headroom (e.g. DeepSeek 123 vs 109) should be reported as a curve over k with random-k and spread-out baselines, next to top-1. / Human agreement numbers are moderate, not near-perfect: weighted kappa of 0.61-0.71 between raters on social acceptability scores (SocNav1), and SocialNav-SUB reports people often disagree. A single acceptable polygon likely understates human spread, especially for groups, which are already weak for every method. / Outside the sources above, in a lab waiting-crowd study (Kupper and Seyfried 2023, J. R. Soc. Interface; 40 to 100 participants per run) neighbour distances sat around 1.0-1.2 m with SD about 0.25 m, inside Hall's personal zone. This is people standing in a waiting crowd, not a robot, but it suggests that the upper end of a 0.5-1.2 m band is the natural spacing.

**Open questions (not checked):** No human study found that measures where people think a robot should stand relative to the last person in a line, at the edge of a conversation group, or next to people watching a screen, with spread reported. The project's rings for these are untested against human choices, as are the mentor's. / No agreement statistic found for click-where-the-robot-should-stop labels. The nearest are rating-based (Cronbach alpha 0.85-0.89 in MANNERS-DB; weighted kappa 0.61-0.71 in SocNav1) or multiple-choice (SocialNav-SUB). The group-joining preprint reports no spread for its 15-person click pilot, nor how many scenes it covered. / The preprint's KL definition (direction, smoothing, zero-bin handling, why values are 14-20) is unspecified, so our numbers could not be compared with theirs without asking the authors. / No pairwise-comparison protocol for choosing among candidate stop places was found for social navigation. A Bradley-Terry fit is standard elsewhere but I found no agreement data for stop-position choices. / No set-valued or conformal coverage-versus-set-size evaluation for robot goal poses was found. Two conformal crowd-navigation papers turned up but I did not read them. / Not confirmed: whether the Francis et al. guidelines' later sections (metrics VI-C to VI-E in full, dataset section IX) say anything on ground-truth validity. I read only part of the document. / Nothing found on how heading-tolerance choices (+/-30 deg in the preprint vs 45 deg here) relate to human tolerance.

- **SocialNav-SUB: Benchmarking VLMs for Scene Understanding in Social Robot Navigation** — LARG group (UT Austin), 2025, CoRL 2025. https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.08757
  - Relevance: It shows a ready way to score a model against a spread of human answers instead of one gold label. It is for fixations and may not transfer fully to click maps.
  - [verified] The paper analyses 8 metrics. They rank models differently because they treat false positives and false negatives differently, account or not for biases, handle spatial deviations differently, and pre-process maps differently.
  - [verified] For a benchmark like the MIT Saliency Benchmark, where models need not be probabilistic but are expected to capture viewing biases, report CC or NSS. - **Evaluation of Trajectory Distribution Predictions with Energy Score** — Shahroudi, Lepson, Kull, 2024, ICML 2024 (PMLR 235). https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/shahroudi24a.html
  - Relevance: This is the best argument for being careful with best-of-k. If our solver returns 5 candidate places, 'any of 5 succeeds' can be gamed by spreading candidates and grows with k, so report hit-at-k as a curve with k stated. The energy score is for continuous trajectory distributions. For our region-based success, the transferable lessons are propriety and cross-k comparability.
  - [verified] Minimum of N (the minADE/minFDE family) is not strictly proper, so a suboptimal forecast can score as well as or better than the Bayes-optimal one.
  - [verified] minADE with L=1 decreases as K increases even when the prediction is unchanged, so it is not comparable across K, whereas the energy score estimates the same expected value for any K.
  - [verified] In a synthetic check, minFDE and FDE optima fall at a distribution with higher variance than the true one, while the energy score is minimized near the truth.
  - [verified] The authors say the metric choice should ideally be informed by the downstream task, and that there are surely tasks where an MoN metric would be the right one. They do not say which tasks, and they do not say MoN is best for some tasks.
- **Cultural differences in how an engagement-seeking robot should approach a group of people** — University of Twente researchers, 2014, ACM conference, 2014 (DOI 10.1145/2631488.2631499); I read only the Twente abstract page. https://research.utwente.nl/en/publications/cultural-differences-in-how-an-engagement-seeking-robot-should-ap/

  - [verified] Online survey with N = 181 participants in three countries (China, USA, Argentina) judging how a robot should approach a group of people.
  - [verified] Participants generally preferred a robot that stays out of people's intimate space, as expected of a human; Chinese participants judged closer approaches as appropriate more than US participants; Argentinian ratings were closer to the US ones.
